## Overview

**Boone County** is a county in northern Illinois with a population of 53,448 (2020 census). Its county seat is **Belvidere**. The county covers 282 square miles (280.7 sq mi land, 1.3 sq mi water) and is part of the Rockford, Illinois metropolitan area. Boone County was formed on March 3, 1836, from Winnebago County and is named for Kentucky frontiersman Daniel Boone.

Boone County operates under the County Board form of government prescribed by Illinois statute (55 ILCS 5). The County Board serves as the legislative body responsible for policy decisions related to operating county government. The county utilizes a **Committee of the Whole (COTW)** structure, in which the full legislative body sits as a committee for discussion and debate on issues before formal Board action. Two standing committees—Administrative & Legislative, and Finance, Taxation & Salaries—handle most preliminary work.

Elected constitutional officers (Clerk & Recorder, Sheriff, State's Attorney, Coroner, Treasurer, Circuit Clerk) and an appointed Administration department manage day-to-day operations. The county also appoints members to several boards and commissions that handle specialized functions such as planning, zoning, health, and conservation easements.

### Committee of the Whole model

Boone County uses a **Committee of the Whole (COTW)** structure. All members of the County Board are members of each COTW. This structure allows the full Board to discuss and debate issues in committee format. The county has two standing COTWs:

- **COTW – Administrative & Legislative**
- **COTW – Finance, Taxation & Salaries**

All committee meetings are open to the public except for closed sessions. The public is encouraged to attend.
