# Spanish glossary

Read by `ccs translate` and pasted verbatim into every translation prompt, so
this file is the editorial control over the Spanish edition. Edit it freely.
Editing it does **not** re-translate anything already stored — the stored
files are keyed on the English source, not on this file. To apply a change
retroactively, re-run with `--force` (and `--only` to scope the cost).

## Style

- US Spanish, neutral register that reads naturally in the Midwest and to
  Mexican-American readers. Formal: *usted*, never *tú*.
- Plain words. Same rule as the English: report, don't characterize; record the
  vote count, not a theory of the vote.
- Sentence case for headings. `$` amounts, vote counts (`8-0`), percentages and
  ordinance/resolution numbers exactly as in the source.
- Numbers keep their English formatting: *18.5 million* is *18.5 millones*,
  *$1,000* stays *$1,000*.
- Ordinals in a meeting schedule are words: *1st Tuesday* is *el primer martes*,
  *3rd Monday* is *el tercer lunes*.
- Dates in prose: *el 14 de septiembre*. Times: *4:30 p. m.*
- Gender: follow the pronouns the English text already uses. Where it uses
  none, use neutral phrasing (*la alcaldía*, *el personal*, *la presidencia*).
  Never infer a person's gender from their name.
- When the page speaks in the first person, never use a gendered noun for the
  speaker (*creador*, *autor*): write around it (*quien creó*, *escribí*).
- Quotes from speakers are translated; they stay in quotation marks.

## Keep in English

Proper nouns, because they are how a reader finds the thing in the record:

- Names of governments, bodies, committees, commissions, departments, offices
  and agencies (*City Council*, *Committee of the Whole*, *Zoning Board of
  Appeals*, *Boone County Board*, *District 100*).
- People's names, and a title attached to a name (*Mayor Morris*,
  *Superintendent Dr. Shug*). A title used alone is translated.
- Streets, places, businesses, subdivisions, and the names of programs, grants
  and projects.
- Acronyms (*TIF*, *IDOT*, *H-2A*). Spell out the Spanish gloss only where the
  source itself explains the acronym.
- The publication's own name, *The Belvidere Wire*.

A name kept in English still takes a Spanish article in running text, and the
article agrees with the Spanish word for what it is: *el City Council* (concejo),
*la Board of Health* (junta), *el Committee of the Whole* (comité), *la Planning
Commission* (comisión), *el District 100* (distrito). Never drop the article.

A body mentioned generically, not by name, is translated: *the council voted*
→ *el concejo votó*; *Council minutes* → *las actas del concejo*.

Titles of things are **translated**, not kept: agenda items, the subject line of
an ordinance or resolution, proclamations, reports, budget and expenditure
line items (*Waive Building Permit Fees* → *Exención de tarifas de permisos de
construcción*). They take sentence case in Spanish, not the English Title Case. Only the proper names inside them stay (*East Perry Street*,
*7-Eleven*, *YMCA*, *Perry Elementary School*). The ordinance or resolution
number stays as written (*Ordenanza #781H*).

A job title alone (*the City Attorney*, *the Chief*) takes neutral phrasing when
one exists (*la oficina del abogado de la ciudad*); when none reads naturally,
keep the English title rather than guess a gender.

## Section headings

The dispatch prompts produce a fixed set of `##` headings. Use exactly these,
so the Spanish site's headings and search results are consistent:

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| The lede | Lo principal |
| Decisions & votes | Decisiones y votos |
| Public comment | Comentario público |
| Discussion items | Asuntos discutidos |
| Discussion items (no vote) | Asuntos discutidos (sin votación) |
| Attendance & housekeeping | Asistencia y asuntos administrativos |
| Notable moments | Momentos destacados |
| Financial / operational notes | Notas financieras y operativas |
| Agenda preview | Adelanto de la agenda |
| Other business | Otros asuntos |
| Items for decision | Asuntos para decisión |

Any other heading: translate it, keeping sentence case. A parenthetical
instruction left in a heading (for example *Decisions & votes (each with vote
count…)*) is translated along with it.
