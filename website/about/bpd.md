## Overview

The Belvidere Township Park District is an independently governed special district established by referendum on November 4, 1919, with 764 votes in favor and 280 opposed. It is a separate governmental unit that levies its own taxes to provide park facilities and recreation for the residents of Belvidere Township in Boone County, Illinois. The district covers Belvidere Township, including land both inside and outside the city limits of Belvidere.

The district was formed as Belvidere's population grew and the need for park spaces increased, "both to provide open recreational land and as a beautifying element to attract new families and businesses." The district made its first land purchase on March 9, 1920—a fifty-five acre parcel known as the Baltic Mill tract—for $14,500. This property included the Mill, a residence on West Lincoln Avenue, the river dam, the mill race, Gooseberry Island, and a parcel of land on the south bank of the Kishwaukee River.

Today, the district holds significantly more land. The sources note that a 90-acre parcel between the Belvidere Cemetery and the Fairgrounds, north of Belvidere, planned for development as Prairie Fields Sport Park, "almost doubles the land holdings of the park district."

## Facilities and programs

**Major facilities and features:**

- **Belvidere Park** – The historic core property, originally 105 acres, including the Baltic Mill (renovated 1995 to include historic artifacts, a meeting room, public restroom, and office space for the Boone County Arts Council), Gooseberry Island (picnic area with access via two suspension bridges), the Kishwaukee Riverfront Path (multi-phase construction beginning 1989, extending from Belvidere Park to the Burgess Street Boat Launch and into Doty Park), Activity Center (shelter and ice skating facility off West Locust Street), and a stage area for "Concerts in the Park"
- **Swimming pool** – Opened June 1941 with WPA funding; renovated in 1984; bathhouse renovated 1994 to accommodate individuals with disabilities; includes a separate children's pool (built 1979) and concession stand
- **Baltic Mill Annex** – 920 W. Lincoln Ave., hosts board meetings
- **Administration Building** – 1006 W. Lincoln Ave., completed 1999
- **Doty Park** – Originally acquired 1920 (three and one-half acres), sold to the school district in 1946, re-acquired by the park district in 1992; revitalized in 1994 to include a handicapped accessible playground, large shelter with restrooms, boat dock, two large open field areas, and connection to the Kishwaukee Riverfront Path via a walking bridge over the Kishwaukee River
- **Embankment Skatepark** – Added to Doty Park in 2000
- **Aberdeen Park** – Ten acres off Orth Road, built outside city limits; includes tennis courts, sand volleyball court, horseshoe pits, shuffleboard court, playground, ball diamond, soccer field, walking path, shelter with restrooms, and two parking lots; property acquired through county land-cash ordinance and developer contributions
- **Fridh Recreation Area** – Located at 9th Street and 14th Avenue; currently includes a soccer field and parking lot; future expansion planned to include playground, ball field, shelter, and restrooms
- **Prairie Fields Sport Park** – 90-acre parcel between Belvidere Cemetery and the Fairgrounds, north of Belvidere; plans include soccer fields, baseball and softball diamonds, senior activity area, shelters, playgrounds, concessions, sand volleyball courts, basketball courts, tennis courts, restrooms, batting cages, neighborhood park, and a path looping the sports park; described as "final plans are underway to begin construction in the near future"
- **Harkless Park** – Conveyed to the park district by the city in 1979
- **Soccer fields on Town Hall Road** – Leased and developed beginning 1986
- **Red Horse Bend** – Canoe launch on the north bank of the Kishwaukee River just east of Belvidere
- **Hickory Bill's Island** – Canoe launch west of the park off the Appleton Road bridges, finished 1998; includes picnic site, gradual path to launch area, and large parking area
- **Burgess Street Boat Launch** – Built 1988 using the district's first state grant

**School partnerships:**

The district worked with schools in the 1990s to fund playgrounds at Perry, Caledonia, Lincoln, Washington, Kishwaukee, and Logan Schools. The district contributed toward construction of the gym and recreational facilities at Meehan School in exchange for after-hours and summer use of those facilities for community programs.

**Historical note on Kiddieland:** A popular playground since the late 1960s, noted as inaccessible to many Belvidere children, which prompted the school partnership strategy.

## The Parks & Conservation Foundation

The Parks & Conservation Foundation is a separate 501(c)(3) organization established in 1992 to assist the Belvidere Park District and the Boone County Conservation District to acquire and develop land. The foundation is "completely independent from the park board" and has its own members and by-laws.

The foundation holds title to over $1 million worth of land acquired through grants. Recent projects include building the playground in Bob's Park. All gifts to the foundation are tax deductible. The foundation will work with landowners who wish to have land preserved in its natural state.

Daniel Noble, the current Board President, is listed as a board member of the Parks & Conservation Foundation. Mary Marquardt, Board Secretary, is listed as an active member of the foundation.
