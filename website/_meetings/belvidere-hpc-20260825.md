## The lede

- The commission approved designating Perry School at 633 W. Perry Street as a local landmark after a public hearing, recognizing the 1897 building as one of the oldest operational schools in Illinois
- The commission authorized a $1,500 property maintenance grant reimbursement to St. John's United Church of Christ for new window installation
- Mayor Clint Morris applied for the Perry School landmark designation, emphasizing the building's historical significance to the city and neighborhood
- The commission moved forward with plans for a Victorian Picnic fundraiser scheduled for September 26, 2026, at the Swift House

## Decisions & votes

The commission approved the minutes from the July 28, 2026 meeting with a 7-0 voice vote.

After a public hearing, the commission approved the landmark designation for Perry School at 633 W. Perry Street with a 7-0 roll call vote. The hearing began at 5:33 pm and closed at 5:53 pm. Staff planner Gina DelRose testified that the public hearing notice was published in the Belvidere Daily Republican on August 7, 2026.

The commission approved a $1,500 property maintenance grant reimbursement to St. John's United Church of Christ at 401 N. Main Street for window installation with a 6-0-1 roll call vote. Dawn Snow abstained.

## Discussion items

During the Perry School landmark hearing, Mayor Clint Morris stated "there is nothing more important than preserving Perry School" and read a narrative into the record stressing the property is "a big part of the City's history and the neighborhood."

Vice Chair David Kummerow asked if the property owner understood what landmarking entails. DelRose explained the restrictions on exterior alterations, the certificate of appropriateness process, and available tax credits.

Kummerow also questioned whether neighbors were notified of the landmark request. DelRose clarified that state statute only requires newspaper publication for public hearings, while neighbor notification applies only to zoning matters before the Planning and Zoning Commission.

DelRose reported that St. John's UCC had installed new windows and the results "look great."

For the upcoming 2026 Awards Program, Chair Sonya Dobberfuhl requested DelRose send a list of past winners and properties discussed for this year. Commissioners are expected to bring nominations to the September meeting.

DelRose reported she found bags and stickers online for the scavenger hunt museum bags and will be ordering them. Commissioner Kim Coniglio distributed Texas Roadhouse peanuts for the Chef's Night fundraiser.

DelRose said she is waiting to advertise the Victorian Picnic until after the county fair to avoid the event getting lost in other marketing. Natali Ramboldt noted she will be at the museum for the Quilt Show closing that day, and Carl Swanson said he would be out of town.

David Kummerow announced that the first episode of America's Scariest Houses will air on September 18, 2026.

DelRose gave an overview of local projects.

## Financial / operational notes

The commission authorized a $1,500 reimbursement to St. John's United Church of Christ for window installation completed under the Historic Property Maintenance Program.

Perry School was built in 1897 and originally offered eight grades. In its final year of operation it housed grades K through 5. The staff report noted it is believed to be the second oldest continuously operating school building in Illinois, after Allen Grade School in Bureau, built in 1887.

The school was named after Commodore Matthew Calbraith Perry, who in July 1853 sailed into Tokyo Bay and opened feudal Japan to international commerce. The school was built to meet growth demands from the National Sewing Machine Company, which located in Belvidere in 1886 and expanded in 1894. Belvidere's population grew 31% from 1880 to 1890 and another 79% from 1890 to 1900.

The property is zoned I, Institutional District, and designated as Public/Semi-Public in the city's comprehensive plan adopted May 7, 2024. The property encompasses eight parcels.

The staff report noted that Perry School has remained largely unchanged, unlike Lincoln School (demolished) and Logan School (which received historically inappropriate additions). The building features a gray cut stone foundation topped by brown brick, oculi bracketing the front entrance, and diamond-checked brickwork on the front gable.
