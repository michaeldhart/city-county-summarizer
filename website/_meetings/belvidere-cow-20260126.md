## The lede

- Fire Department to transition compliance reporting from Brycer to First Due software at no cost to the city; businesses pay $20 per submission
- City accepted $1,000 donation from Midwest Refrigerated Services for Eagle Scout Santa Claus House project
- Approved Federal Railroad Administration grant agreement for $1,666,279 for Appleton Road grade separation preliminary engineering and environmental review
- Awarded bid of $1,380,432.25 to Fisher Excavating for Parking Lot #7 expansion and Kishwaukee Overlook project

## Discussion items

**Police Department staffing update**: Chief Woody reported department now at 43 officers (three recently sworn in attending academy), leaving one vacancy to reach authorized strength. Five officers in field training program, two on leave. Interviewing lateral applicant and four entry-level candidates this week. Monica Hernandez hired as new administrative assistant to investigations.

**Fire Department operations**: Chief Schadle reported 304 calls in past 30 days, including 7 fires (4 structure fires), 21 hazardous situations, 193 medical calls. Notable: 26 falls and 28 lift assists (nearly 20% of total calls), attributed to winter weather. National reporting system changed from NFIRS to NEIRS (National Emergency Incident Reporting System). Ambulance service has transported 5,437 patients since inception.

**Compliance reporting transition rationale**: First Due integration will improve efficiency by consolidating inspection and compliance data in single platform, eliminate duplicate records, allow department control over communications to businesses, and reduce costs for businesses submitting multiple reports.

**Budget work session reminder**: Finance Director Turnipseed announced budget work session scheduled for Saturday, February 21st at 8:30 a.m. at City Hall.

**Ward boundary updates**: City Attorney Drella clarified Chapter 34 amendments are purely administrative cleanup to reflect annexations already incorporated into wards; no actual boundary changes proposed.

## Notable moments

Chief Schadle noted the federal fire incident reporting system changed from NFIRS (National Fire Incident Reporting System, in use since 1974) to NEIRS (National Emergency Incident Reporting System) in 2026, with the notable omission of "Fire" from the title despite tracking fire incidents. The new system offers real-time reporting versus the previous 40-day delay.

Director Anderson acknowledged the Appleton Road grade separation project has been needed "since 1966" — noting the city is "a few years behind, but we'll get there." The project supports vehicle production at the Stellantis Belvidere Assembly Plant and is expected to reduce delays, improve emergency response times, and lower emissions from idling vehicles at railroad crossings.

Chief Schadle expressed relief that despite extremely cold weather, the department did not see elevated carbon monoxide incidents from overworked furnaces or space heater fires, despite increased use of alternate heating sources.
