## The lede

- The city council approved routine expenditures totaling $5,060,456.60 and held first readings of six ordinances, including the annual tax levy and development fee adjustments, with second readings scheduled for December 7th.
- The council approved a 30% increase in health insurance costs, selecting UnitedHealthcare at approximately $751,184 more than the current year after determining the competing Blue Cross Blue Shield bid would cost employees more out-of-pocket.
- Mayor Clinton Morris addressed the council and public in a lengthy statement defending his communication with DataWatt Energy regarding the controversial "Project Hawkeye" data center, explaining that his role required listening to any proposal and gathering facts, not making unilateral decisions.
- The council appointed Jeff Peters to fill the unexpired term of Planning & Zoning Commissioner Megan English, whose term runs through June 30, 2027.

## Discussion items

**Health insurance renewal discussion:**  
City Attorney Mike Drella explained that four major insurers were approached; Cigna and Aetna declined to bid. UHC renewal represents a 30% increase ($751,184 annually, approximately $28,000 per employee) but was chosen over Blue Cross Blue Shield's 35% increase ($873,991 annually, $29,000 per employee). The BCBS plan design was inferior, with higher employee out-of-pocket costs for copays, out-of-network care, and medications, and would have cost the city significantly more to self-fund benefits down to levels required by collective bargaining agreements.

**Mayor's statement on DataWatt Energy / Project Hawkeye:**  
Mayor Morris delivered an extended statement in response to public requests that he speak. He explained that his role requires meeting with any entity approaching the city and gathering facts before bringing proposals to the council for a vote. He said he never received complete factual information from DataWatt despite repeatedly requesting it. He defended the $150,000 paid by DataWatt as funds to allow independent third-party vetting rather than taxpayer dollars. He addressed criticism about his attention during a City-County Coordinating Committee meeting, explaining he was present only as a courtesy for the committee's first meeting after he helped reinstitute it, was not on the agenda, and was simply checking messages on his phone. He stated that a council member (later context suggests Alderman Stevens) received a death threat over his position on the data center issue. He said the resolution passed by the council on November 2nd directing him to cease negotiations was "not even legal" and "non-binding." He asked for civility and said he represents all 25,336 Belvidere residents, not just those attending meetings.

**Public comment themes:**  
Ten residents spoke during public comment, all opposing data centers. Comments focused on water contamination concerns (citing an Oregon case study linking Amazon data centers to nitrate-contaminated drinking water), noise impacts, agricultural land preservation, wildlife and environmental damage, health concerns including rare cancers, lack of community benefit, and criticism of the mayor for not publicly opposing the project.

## Financial / operational notes

The 2027 health insurance costs represent a significant budget increase. The UHC plan at $28,000 per employee annually requires the city to self-fund higher deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums down to amounts set in collective bargaining agreements.

The payroll expenditures in the bills list show wages, benefits, pension contributions, and payroll taxes for the October 23 and November 6, 2026 pay periods totaling $872,885.85 from the payroll fund.

Water/Sewer fund major capital expenditures included $987,387.18 to L&L Builders for pay application #10 on the Well 11 facility construction and $145,869.34 to Baxter & Woodman for 2027 wastewater treatment plant improvements.

The monthly financial, departmental, and revenue reports for October 2026 were placed on file at the City Clerk's office.

## Public comment

**Ruth Haberstitch** cited a recent investigative report linking Amazon Web Services data centers in Oregon to concentrated nitrate contamination in household drinking water wells, with levels up to seven times EPA limits, and clusters of rare cancers, non-Hodgkin's lymphomas, thyroid disorders, and miscarriages. She accused local government of withholding information from the public, calling it "deception."

**Vivian Penny** (436 Andrews Drive) reminded the mayor that he had justified the single garbage hauler requirement as reducing neighborhood noise to one day per week, and questioned why he now seemed unconcerned about the constant, much louder noise a data center would create. She asked if he would move away if a data center comes.

**Deborah Wilson** asked whether the data center company or the city's taxpayers would pay for water system upgrades, and who would cover medical bills for residents with smoke allergies affected by emissions.

**Teresa Stein** (1018 East Lincoln Avenue) said she lives far from the proposed data center site but hears it will create problems for the entire town. She criticized the mayor for not speaking to residents about the issue and said it was "not acceptable" for a mayor to remain silent.

**Leanne Newman** (8669 Centaur Drive, Belvidere) described her family's 19.33-acre property on Fern Hill Road in Spring Township, which includes woods with native wildflowers and diverse wildlife. She said she values the land for her three grandchildren and her son, who purchased adjacent property to farm mini cows and become independent despite medical issues. She has received offers of $40,000 per acre for solar development but refuses to sell. She invited council members to visit the property to see it is not flat farmland but includes significant wooded areas and habitat.

**Adam David Newman** (4234 Fern Hill Road) said Illinois loses approximately 77,000 acres of farmland annually and once it's gone it doesn't come back, which will raise food and fuel prices. He said he sees no positives to the data center proposal.

**Rebecca Meyer** (3097 Garden Prairie Road) thanked the eight aldermen who voted for Resolution 2026-24 to end negotiations with DataWatt Energy, particularly commending Rick Brereton for drafting an ordinance to ban data centers. She criticized Mayor Morris for writing an enthusiastic letter of support to DataWatt, remaining on his phone during public comment, and failing to keep campaign promises to put taxpayers first and provide transparency. She said no residents have spoken in support of Project Hawkeye and urged the council to pass permanent ordinances banning hyperscale data centers.

**Michael Nowicki** thanked residents for attending meeting after meeting and doing research. He said if the council cannot see the entire community opposes the project, "then you're blind." He said economic reasons should not outweigh neighbors being unable to stay in their homes, and the project affects the entire county, not just Belvidere.

## Notable moments

Mayor Morris made his first extended public statement on the DataWatt Energy controversy, responding to weeks of public criticism about his silence. His remarks lasted several minutes and directly addressed accusations about his communications with the company, the $150,000 payment, his attention during public meetings, and his view of the council's November 2nd resolution.

The mayor revealed that a council member (later identified contextually as Alderman Stevens) received a death threat that "they were going to shoot him" over his position on the data center issue. The mayor said he has also been threatened.

The mayor described the November 2nd council resolution directing him to cease negotiations with DataWatt as "not even legal" and "non-binding," saying it was "eye wash."

The mayor criticized "extreme rhetoric" from data center opponents, referencing previous public comments that accused data center supporters of being "baby murderers or child pedophile supporters," which he said received applause from some in the audience.

Alderman John Albertini challenged the mayor after his statement, saying he found it "highly offensive" that the mayor called out only himself, Stevens, and Peterson by name, stating "the other eight of us are also doing our due diligence."

Alderwoman Sandra Gramkowski stated there is no due diligence possible without an actual site plan, geotechnical engineering, and electrical engineering, and said up to now "it's just emotion and respond from emotion."

The mayor said DataWatt proposed using a closed-loop water system, contradicting claims the facility would use 3.5 to 5 million gallons of water per day.

Six ordinances received first reading with no discussion: development fee amendments, sale of surplus vehicles and equipment, the annual tax levy, two special service area levies for Farmington Ponds subdivisions, and a special use permit for a daycare center at 390 West Chrysler Drive.
