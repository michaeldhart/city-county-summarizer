## The lede

- The county board approved a $650,717.81 claims payment and a courthouse construction change order that resulted in a $225.42 credit to the county after extensive negotiation on masonry restoration costs
- Dozens of residents packed the meeting to voice concerns about a proposed massive data center project (Project Hawk Eye) in the area, though Chairman clarified the county currently has no jurisdiction over the project and data centers are not even recognized as an allowable use in county code
- The board discussed the fiscal year 2027 budget, which includes a 2.98% property tax levy increase and a $200,000 transfer to cover overruns in the jail's arrestee medical costs fund
- Sheriff Dave Ernest announced the retirement of Sergeant Bill Kaiser after 22 years and reported five traffic fatalities since his last appearance, including a crash where first responders saved a 4-year-old child
- The board is considering memorials at the courthouse to honor former longtime public servants including former Board Chairman Don Meyer, who passed away October 16 at age 91 after decades of service

## Attendance & housekeeping

Members present: Carl Johnson, Josh Schumacher, Brian Schneider, David Wiltse, Tom Walber, Rodney Riley, Marion Thornberry, and Dana Langston (attending electronically).

Members absent: David Cordrey, Lisa Patterson, and Matt Organo.

The meeting began at 6:05 p.m. with a brief delay due to technical difficulties. The board approved the agenda and the October 1, 2026 meeting minutes.

## Decisions & votes

**Motion to allow Dana Langston to attend electronically:** Approved by voice vote with no opposition. Moved by Schumacher, seconded by Walber.

**Motion to approve October 1, 2026 meeting minutes:** Approved by voice vote with no opposition. Moved by Thornberry, seconded by Riley.

**Motion to approve claims totaling $650,717.81:** Approved by roll call vote (8-0). Moved by Riley, seconded by Walber. Carl Johnson abstained from voting on claims presented by William Charles, Northern Illinois Service Co., Core & Main, all Boone County Highway Department claims, and any county department claims regarding sewage, storm water, construction, and excavation. The amount was corrected from an initially stated $660,992.81.

**Motion to approve Change Order #33 for courthouse project:** Approved by roll call vote (8-0). Moved by Wiltse, seconded by Schneider. The change order amount was revised during discussion from a cost of $11,393.31 to a credit of $225.42. The change order included five separate items: refrigeration piping insulation ($2,619.01 addition, an engineering error to be recovered from RTM), civil engineering work for sidewalk ($3,794.20), masonry restoration mortar upgrade ($4,338.98, negotiated down from over $20,000), window film for State's Attorney office ($641.08), and mortar mockups with delay costs ($6,440). Credits included baffle lobby lights ($575.70), floor boxes ($1,332.19), and handrail material change from stainless steel to galvanized ($20,000).

**Motion to move telecom audit proposal to county board:** Approved by voice vote with no opposition. Moved by Riley, seconded by Thornberry. The proposal would hire BHG Consulting to audit phone lines and billing, with the company receiving 35% of recovered funds and the county keeping 65%. The county currently spends almost $10,000 monthly on Frontier services.

**Motion to move 2027 holiday schedule to county board:** Approved by voice vote with no opposition. Moved by Wiltse, seconded by Schumacher. The schedule includes 13 holidays and matches Winnebago County's schedule for court coordination.

**Motion to enter executive session for personnel matters:** Approved by roll call vote (8-0). Moved by Schneider, seconded by Wiltse. Executive session began at 7:17 p.m.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**Data center concerns:** Chairman explained the county's limited jurisdiction over data centers. He clarified that data centers are not currently recognized as an allowable use in county code, and any developer would need to complete a lengthy text amendment process before even applying for a specific project. He noted that most data centers annex to municipalities, removing them from county jurisdiction entirely. No applications or inquiries have been received by the county.

**FY 2027 Budget:** The board reviewed the proposed budget, which includes a 2.98% property tax levy increase (below the statutory maximum of 3.3%). Notable items include a $30,000 increase to Regional Office of Education (bringing total to $140,000, a 22% increase that the county is statutorily required to pay), $100,000 increase for IT department (including a new employee), a $1 million transfer to the Property Maintenance Fund for jail equipment, courthouse roof study and repairs, and Public Safety Building improvements. A $200,000 transfer will address the deficit in the Arrestee Medical Costs Fund. The coroner's salary remains unchanged due to statutory timing requirements for elected official salary adjustments. The budget also includes $5,000 additional for University of Illinois Extension and $10,000 for Senior Connections.

**December meeting schedule change:** The organizational meeting will be December 7 at 6:30 p.m., with administration and finance committees meeting back-to-back on December 10 at 6:00 p.m. The board meeting will be December 17.

**2026-2027 meeting schedule:** Discussion postponed to allow the incoming board to set their own schedule, as four current members will not be returning.

**Memorial proposals for courthouse:** Administration proposed establishing memorials for longtime public servants, particularly Don Meyer (former board chairman who passed October 16, 2026), Julie Clivy (former circuit clerk with over 60 years service), Linda Anderson (over 40 years in circuit clerk's office, passed 2024), and Sylvia Schroder (over 40 years in county clerk's office, passed 2018). One suggestion involved dedicating trees being planted at the courthouse with plaques. The board was asked to consider ideas and provide feedback.

**Courthouse roof study:** A study on the remaining courthouse roof sections, including the skylight, was completed last week with results expected by end of the week. Options will be provided.

## Public comment

**Lisa Fitzgerald, 2913 Carlson Road:** Expressed concern about the proposed data center project and its potential impacts on residents, including dry wells, water contamination, constant noise, health effects, and property value loss. Asked the board to stand up for county residents and provide accountability if the project moves forward, noting residents feel helpless and lied to. Referenced a $150,000 check received by the mayor that was not disclosed to aldermen.

**Rebecca Meyer, 3097 Garden Prairie Road:** Directly challenged District 1 board members (Schneider, Cordrey, Schumacher, and Wiltse) for remaining silent on Project Hawk Eye, a proposed 3-gigawatt hyperscale data center by Data Watt Energy on Genoa Road. Criticized their lack of vocal opposition despite concerns about power infrastructure strain, water supply drain, and industrial noise. Called on them to formally object to the project.

**Randy Fenzel, 10121 Crawford Road, Garden Prairie:** Suggested the county could make it impractical for data centers to operate by passing ordinances restricting noise after 6:00 p.m., limiting light pollution, and controlling air pollution that crosses property lines. Reminded board members that residents "can vote you out, too."

**Ruth Haberstick, 8227 Fruit Farm Road:** Read from Google AI research explaining how tech companies use psychological tactics to persuade local governments, including economic promises, green branding, and job inflation. Noted that if Belvidere backs out, developers could still work with the county board since the site is on unincorporated land.

**Margaret (last name unclear), 4777 Stone Quarry Road:** Described impacts of data centers on horses, including disrupted breeding due to stress from low-frequency noise, construction traffic, and nighttime lighting. Explained horses can develop gastric ulcers and colic from stress, and sudden noises from generator tests can cause them to bolt through fences. Ground vibrations during construction would create panic. Asked board to work with residents and vote against any data center proposal.

**Wayne Roloff, 3590 Shattuck Road, Garden Prairie:** Criticized the chairman for "scolding" residents "like unruly third graders" for applauding at meetings. Argued that 30-40 taxpaying families who pay $300,000-$400,000 annually deserve respect. Described the proposed data center as "approximately one third larger than Chrysler, the refrigeration complex, and our Walmart store combined" and "one of, if not the largest data center in the world." Called it "Morris's folly" and insisted residents will continue to applaud speakers at meetings they fund with their taxes.

## Notable moments

Sheriff Dave Ernest delivered an emotional farewell to departing board members, particularly Carl Johnson and Dana Langston, thanking them for their service and noting that their work often goes unrecognized despite long meeting nights. He said his 130 employees appreciated their dedication.

Chairman opened the meeting by addressing the large crowd about data centers before public comment began, providing a detailed explanation of county jurisdiction and the regulatory process, which drew audible reactions from the audience when he stated the county has "nothing to do with any of it at this point."

Chairman asked the audience multiple times to refrain from applauding during public comment, comparing the meeting room to a "gymnasium" and requesting respect for the process.

Sheriff Ernest shared a moving account of a citizen and deputy who saved a 4-year-old child at the scene of a fatal crash on Poplar Grove Road that killed two young adults. The citizen cut the child from the car seat, and a deputy ran up the road to meet the arriving ambulance.

The meeting included a somber tribute to Don Meyer, the longest-serving Boone County Board Chairman (1986-2002), who passed away October 16 at age 91. Chairman credited Meyer with hiring the county's first administrator, Ken Terrinoni, during a financially difficult period.

Sheriff Ernest reported that tomorrow the department will transport 11 inmates to the Department of Corrections—an unusually high number that typically ranges from three to four—crediting the corrections staff and State's Attorney's Office for the strong relationship with IDOC.

The meeting revealed ongoing financial pressure from the Regional Office of Education, with a 22% budget increase (from $110,000 to $140,000) that board members called "atrocious" and "ludicrous," though the county is statutorily required to pay the amount with no say in ROE spending decisions.
