## Agenda preview

## The lede

- The Belvidere Committee of the Whole will review a proposed 20-year moratorium on data centers, citing concerns about noise, water use, agricultural land, and limited job creation.
- The city will consider purchasing two ambulances for $560,840 rather than continuing to lease them from Metro Paramedic Services, a move the Fire Chief says would save $169,362 over six years.
- The Public Works Department is seeking approval for two change orders totaling $55,361 for Well #11 construction—one to add a required water flow meter and another to relocate a pressure relief valve.
- Aldermen will receive updates from the Police and Fire departments and discuss disposal of surplus fire equipment including expired grenade cases, broken lights, and damaged office chairs.
- The committee may enter closed session to discuss collective bargaining matters.

## Discussion items

**Public Safety**

- Police Department update
- Fire Department update
- Ambulance procurement: Fire Chief proposes the city own two new Type III ambulances rather than lease them through Metro. Purchasing from North Central Emergency Vehicles would cost $1,151,310 over six fiscal years (FY29-FY34) compared to $1,320,672 to lease through Metro. The city would own the ambulances, two cardiac monitors, two LUCAS devices, medical and communication equipment, and a spare ambulance. Three bids were received, with North Central's $560,840 proposal the lowest. Payment would be structured as a down payment in FY29 from the Capital Fund, with the remaining $675,645 financed over five years at an estimated 4.8% rate. Delivery is scheduled for February 1, 2028, ahead of the May 1, 2028 service date when the current Metro agreement ends.
- Disposal of surplus Fire Department property: The Fire Chief requests authorization to dispose of expired Stat-X grenade cases, nonworking scene lights, a nonfunctioning hose tester, an unused flotation device launcher, used hand tools, a surplus SCUBA tank, two damaged office chairs, a surplus ice rescue board, and a nonfunctioning printer. Items with no value would be properly disposed of; items with potential value would be offered for sale.

**Finance & Personnel**

- Finance Department update

**Other Business**

- Ordinance placing a moratorium on data centers: A proposed 20-year ban on leasing, zoning, approving, permitting, and constructing data centers within city limits, citing concerns about agricultural land use, noise from cooling systems and diesel generators reaching 100 decibels, massive utility demands threatening the aquifer and potentially raising electricity costs, and minimal permanent job creation despite large physical footprints. The moratorium would run through November 6, 2046.
- Well #11 Facility Construction Change Order #15: Adds a 12-inch raw water flow meter inadvertently omitted when the water storage tank was removed from the project. The meter is required to meet Illinois Environmental Protection Agency reporting requirements. Cost is $27,718.44, to be covered by IEPA funds.
- Well #11 Facility Construction Change Order #16: Relocates the pressure relief valve from downstream to between the well and filter to protect against excessive pressure from "dead-head" conditions. Cost is $27,643, to be covered by IEPA funds.
- Executive session pursuant to Section 2(c)(2) of the Open Meetings Act to discuss collective bargaining matters

## Financial / operational notes

**Ambulance procurement:** The Fire Chief's memo compares leasing versus owning ambulances. Metro's lease pricing is fixed only for contract terms—Elmhurst saw a 31% increase on renewal in 2022, and Wheaton's Metro contract increased 17.8% in one year despite calling for 3% annual increases. Metro agreements can be terminated on 180 days' notice, less than the 14-month procurement time for ambulances. The agreement includes a force majeure clause covering staffing shortages. City ownership would lock in costs and ensure equipment remains in Belvidere if the contract ends. Supporting documents show examples from Elmhurst (where ambulance fees were raised to offset Metro contract increases) and Wheaton (where paramedic wages were increased to $70,021 starting salary in year 3 to address nationwide paramedic shortages).

**Well #11 construction:** Both change orders will be funded by IEPA. Change Order #15 adds a flow meter required for regulatory compliance. Change Order #16 relocates safety equipment to prevent potential equipment damage or catastrophic failure. Lead times noted: flow meter 27 working days, pressure transmitter 25 working days, with 4 days for installation, totaling 36 days schedule impact for Change Order #15; 32 days impact for Change Order #16.
