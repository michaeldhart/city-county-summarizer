## The lede

- The board approved the final fiscal year 2027 budget projecting a $10.9 million deficit, with officials warning the district could face cash flow problems by FY28 and exhaust reserves by 2030-2031 if deficit spending continues
- The board held public hearings on a proposed $18.5 million bond issuance for fire prevention and safety improvements and the final FY27 budget, with one resident criticizing the district's planned facilities spending
- Belvidere High School received recognition as a Promising Practices Professional Learning Community from Solution Tree for documented success in raising student achievement
- The board approved the 2026-2027 district scorecard and superintendent goals as the district prepares to revise its strategic plan based on the portrait of a graduate developed last year
- Sixth-day enrollment figures showed 6,907 students, down from 7,195 in 2023, with an unexpectedly significant drop in kindergarten enrollment raising concerns about future funding

## Attendance & housekeeping

All seven board members were present: Barbi Yunk, Jermaine Pigee, Scott Mueller, Holly Houk, Jorge Herrera (President), Jeff Diestelmeier, and Brian Hoemke.

The meeting began at 6:11 p.m. following closed session. Students from Belvidere North High School performed a scene from their upcoming production of "Clue" before the regular meeting commenced. The board approved the agenda as presented with no amendments.

## Decisions & votes

**BINA Public Hearing on Bond Issuance (closed)** — The board closed the public hearing on the proposed $18.5 million fire prevention and safety bond issuance after one public comment. No vote was required at this stage.

**Public Hearing on FY27 Budget (closed)** — The board closed the public hearing on the final FY27 budget with no public comments received.

**Board Protocols (approved 7-0)** — The board approved updated board protocols developed during their August self-evaluation retreat. Vote: Houk, Hoemke, Mueller, Pigee, Yunk, Diestelmeier, Herrera all voted yes.

**Policy Revisions (approved 7-0)** — The board approved revisions to six existing policies: 2:230 Public Participation at School Board Meetings, 5:40 Communicable and Chronic Infectious Disease, 5:240 Suspension, 7:30 Student Assignment and Intra-District Transfer, 7:330 Student Use of Buildings - Equal Access, and 8:70 Accommodating Individuals with Disabilities. Vote: Diestelmeier, Yunk, Hoemke, Pigee, Mueller, Houk, Herrera all voted yes.

**Orland Park Dental Services Agreement (approved 7-0)** — The board approved the annual agreement for Orland Park Dental Services to provide free dental care to qualifying students. The agreement includes a change to use silver diamine fluoride. Discussion noted increasing student participation and the service's importance for students who otherwise wouldn't access dental care. Vote: Houk, Diestelmeier, Mueller, Yunk, Hoemke, Pigee, Herrera all voted yes.

**District Scorecard (approved 7-0)** — The board approved the 2026-2027 school district scorecard tracking key performance indicators aligned to strategic plan goals. Dr. Carson explained the scorecard serves as a tool to monitor progress and will be updated as the district develops a revised strategic plan this year. Vote: Hoemke, Houk, Diestelmeier, Mueller, Yunk, Pigee, Herrera all voted yes.

**Annual Fuel Bid (approved 7-0)** — The board approved the annual fuel bid as presented. Member Herrera questioned whether bidding could be moved to winter months when fuel prices are typically lower rather than summer; Mrs. Armstrong said they could request an extension to shift the timing. Vote: Mueller, Hoemke, Yunk, Houk, Pigee, Diestelmeier, Herrera all voted yes.

**FY27 Copy Paper Bid (approved 7-0)** — The board approved the fiscal year 2027 copy paper bid. Vote: Hoemke, Mueller, Houk, Yunk, Pigee, Diestelmeier, Herrera all voted yes.

**Final FY27 Budget (approved 7-0)** — The board approved the district's final fiscal year 2027 budget projecting a $10.9 million deficit. Extensive discussion covered healthcare cost increases of 38% this year alone (with benefits costs rising 59.7% over five years), declining enrollment from 7,195 to 6,907 students, underfunded state transportation and special education mandates, tax levy caps limiting revenue options, and the district's fund balance declining to approximately 126 days cash on hand. Mrs. Armstrong emphasized the district cannot continue deficit spending and would face cash flow problems in FY28 and exhaust reserves by 2030-2031 without substantial reductions. Board members asked detailed questions about healthcare costs, tax rate caps, dues and fees line items, and the timeline for state funding based on enrollment. President Herrera summarized that difficult spending reductions are imperative for the district's long-term financial health. Vote: Hoemke, Pigee, Houk, Yunk, Diestelmeier, Mueller, Herrera all voted yes.

**IASB Delegate (approved 7-0)** — The board appointed Member Pigee as the district's delegate to the Illinois Association of School Boards Delegate Assembly with authority to vote on behalf of the board on resolutions at the November conference. Vote: Diestelmeier, Mueller, Pigee, Hoemke, Yunk, Houk, Herrera all voted yes.

**Superintendent Goals (approved 7-0)** — The board approved Dr. Schug's 2026-2027 superintendent goals including task-oriented goals around budget planning, facilities master plan implementation, strategic plan continuation, family and community partnerships, and multilingual program review, plus data-oriented goals targeting graduation rates, math and ELA achievement, and achievement gaps for special education and English learner students. Vote: Hoemke, Houk, Diestelmeier, Mueller, Yunk, Pigee, Herrera all voted yes.

**Consent Agenda (approved 7-0)** — The board approved consent items including minutes, personnel report, financial reports, bills, Microsoft renewal, intergovernmental agreement for Caledonia access road, excess property disposals, overnight field trip request, facility use request, fundraisers, and donations. Member Herrera questioned a $60,000 Nexus payment on the bills, seeking clarification on whether it was for phase one or phase two facilities work; Mrs. Armstrong and representatives confirmed all current charges are for phase one only with no phase two billing until projects are approved. Vote: Yunk, Pigee, Diestelmeier, Hoemke, Houk, Mueller, Herrera all voted yes.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**Board Protocols** — Dr. Schug presented updated board protocols developed during the board's self-evaluation retreat for discussion before formal approval. Board members indicated general agreement with the revisions. Dr. Schug noted they are working to schedule another self-evaluation session for March 2027.

**IASB Delegate Assembly** — Dr. Schug explained the process for appointing a delegate to vote on resolutions at the IASB conference. Member Pigee volunteered to serve again, noting he enjoys the opportunity to make decisions at the state level and communicate with other district delegates. The board discussed allowing the delegate to vote independently on resolutions or providing input; past practice has been for board members to contact the delegate if they have concerns about specific resolutions.

**Upcoming Contract Renewals** — Dr. Schug noted administration is working to add contracts exceeding $25,000 to a list for regular board review.

**First Reading of Policies** — The board received first readings of Policy 6:190 Extracurricular and Co-Curricular Activities and Policy 6:310 High School Credit for Non-District Experiences; Course Substitutions; Re-Entering Students. These policies will return for final approval in October.

## Public comment

**Bill Pyson** criticized the proposed $18.5 million bond as "only the beginning" of over $100 million in planned facilities spending over five years, more than twice the cost of the high school that required a sales tax to pay off. He said taxpayers expect relief as existing bonds mature, not new borrowing. He called on board members to "look yourself in the mirror" and questioned spending $750,000 on a facilities study that wasn't approved ahead of time, referencing an hour-long discussion from two years ago that was "miraculously murked out" of the recording.

**Matt Zickert** addressed budget efficiencies and parameters, saying he warned the board three years ago about the current financial situation. He expressed concern that efficiency measures like not hiring substitutes for co-taught classes and limiting athletic participation directly affect students, especially those with the most needs. He noted inconsistent policies over 20 years regarding teacher coursework reimbursement and questioned items like eliminating plug-in deodorizers and smart passes as cost savings. He referenced the lack of transparency around the facilities plan spending and hoped for "vigorous discussion" in upcoming board elections, saying people need to understand the district's debt direction rather than hearing "it's not costing the taxpayers any money."

## Notable moments

A theatrical performance by Belvidere North High School students opened the meeting before official business began. The cast performed a dinner party scene from their upcoming production of "Clue," introducing their characters and building suspense before plugging their October performances. Board members and audience applauded enthusiastically.

During budget discussion, extensive technical questions led to detailed explanations about fund accounting, with Mrs. Armstrong walking board members through complex line items including the elimination of the tort fund and its merger into the education fund, healthcare premium increases, and the relationship between property tax caps and different fund levies.

President Herrera delivered an unusually direct summary near the end of budget discussion, stating the board is "in a very unenviable position" with declining state funding, property tax caps, no community appetite for tax increases, and alarming fund balance decline, concluding "we just have no other choice" but to make substantial spending reductions because "we as a board have to be responsible for the long-term financial health of the district."

Mrs. Armstrong repeatedly emphasized the district put 72% of its operating budget toward instruction and instructional support (excluding transportation), calling it "a point of pride" and showing "our level of commitment to students" even while operating at a deficit.

The board took a brief break before entering executive session at 9:47 p.m.
