## The lede

- **Public hearing held on potential closure of Perry Elementary School**, a 127-year-old neighborhood school with approximately 175 students, requiring an estimated $6.8–14 million in repairs and upgrades.
- **Overwhelming community opposition**: over 30 speakers—including students, parents, teachers, and community members—passionately defended Perry, citing small class sizes, strong relationships, walkability, and academic success.
- **Key criticisms raised**: alleged pattern of neglect since 2013, broken promises from a 2024 vote to keep Perry open, questionable survey methodology (only 400 respondents from 44,000 district population), and concerns that closure is tied to funding a new early childhood center.
- **No formal votes taken** at this hearing; final decision scheduled for Thursday, April 9, 2026 board meeting.

## Attendance & housekeeping

**Members present:** Barbi Yunk, Jermaine Pigee, Scott Mueller, Holly Houk, Jorge Herrera (President), Jeff Diestelmeier, Brian Hoemke

**Others present:** Dr. Shaw (Superintendent), approximately 30+ community members who provided public comment

Meeting called to order at 5:30 PM with Pledge of Allegiance. Spanish translation services offered (none requested). President Herrera reminded attendees of meeting norms and board protocol. Meeting adjourned at 7:26 PM.

## Decisions & votes

_No content._

## Discussion items (no vote)

**Presentation on Perry Elementary closure consideration** (Dr. Shaw):
- Perry built 1897 (additions 1954), current enrollment ~175 students
- Phase one improvements approved September 2024: $6.8M for secure entry, ADA compliance, access control, roof restoration
- Potential phase two: additional $7–9M for LED lighting, sprinkler system, HVAC modernization
- Total replacement value: $17.5–18.5M
- Board reconsidering closure due to: total investment costs, declining enrollment projections, capacity at other elementary schools, stakeholder feedback
- Design scheduled for July 2026; board considering closure before committing to significant investment
- FAQ available on district website with ongoing updates

## Public comment

**Over 30 speakers addressed the board, including:**

**Students** (Ryder, Jace, Alana, Emma Ensley, Diana Gillian, others): Expressed love for their school, teachers, and friends; fear of losing their "family"; appreciation for small class sizes and individual attention.

**Parents** (Andrew Kitson, Zachary Fort, Rory Peterson, Megan Speak, Brandon Fitchy, Melissa Williams, Catherine Ensley, Hannah Kael, Teresa Beanik, Monica Bonsteel, others): 
- Challenged survey credibility (only 400 respondents, 66% allegedly non-parents, conducted by Optimasters who said they "can make results whatever you want")
- Questioned why $500,000+ from city land-cash collections earmarked for Perry roof never used
- Argued renovation more cost-effective than building new early childhood center
- Cited pattern of deliberate enrollment reduction and maintenance neglect since 2013
- Noted Perry never received "targeted status" designation—one of only three district schools with this achievement record
- Emphasized walkability, neighborhood stability, parent engagement, property values
- Requested exploration of administrative cost reductions, operational efficiencies, transportation optimization before closing schools

**Teachers/Staff** (Cynthia Anderson, Wendy Kitson, Megan Hirsch, Mike Speak, Ryan Peterson, Susan Thomas, Jen Little, Candy Manaliam, Joan Cameron):
- Described unique family atmosphere where all staff know every student by name
- Highlighted benefits of small class sizes (research-backed, per Tennessee STAR study)
- Detailed post-COVID support that kept students engaged and safe
- Expressed concern about broken 2024 promise to invest in Perry
- Documented timeline of deliberate staffing reductions and denied student transfers since 2022
- Questioned rapid 2-month closure process vs. 2-year deliberation in 2024
- Special education teachers emphasized individualized support possible in small setting, warned of increased transportation costs for students with IEPs

**Community members** (Dan Snow, Julie Scheffler, Veronica Jonas, others):
- Challenged "equity" argument—Perry students thriving, not lacking
- Noted Perry's annual electricity cost ($18K) vs. newer schools ($91K–$170K+)
- Argued closing Perry would harm property values, neighborhood stability
- Compared decision to their own multi-generational district investment

**Key recurring themes:**
- Alleged administrative bloat (17 to 32 admin positions, 73% payroll increase 2022–2025)
- $5.4M to Nexus Solutions consultant questioned
- Boundary maps already redrawn despite no decision
- Principal vacancy left unfilled since November 2025 (vs. immediate replacement at other schools)
- Survey listed Seth Whitman as option but school reportedly full
- Concern this sets precedent for future closures

## Notable moments

- **Multiple young children gave emotional testimony**, including second-grader who couldn't finish and whose mother completed statement: "She doesn't want you to close her school."

- **Teacher Wendy Kitson invited board members** to stand at Perry doors at 7:30 AM to witness morning greeting rituals and after-school playground basketball games with teachers.

- **Parent Hannah Kael disclosed traumatic kindergarten experience** with former teacher Mrs. Green (now at Lincoln) who allegedly isolated her son and recommended medication at age 5, contrasting with subsequent teachers who helped him thrive.

- **Zachary Fort challenged specific cost assumptions**, arguing Perry's ADA grandfathered status saves money, questioning need for drop-off lane ("When was anyone hit by a car in Perry's playground?"), noting Perry has functioned without kitchen for 127 years.

- **Former student Madison Quainter** (BHS graduate, 4.0 GPA) testified Perry built her academic foundation, requested if closure proceeds, board include plaque reading "Rest in Peace, Perry Elementary."

- **Multiple speakers noted they overcame fear of public speaking** because "I have to teach my kids to stand up for what's right."

- **Teacher Susan Thomas provided detailed timeline** documenting systematic enrollment reduction since 2013 Kishwaukee closure, forced teacher transfers, denied student registrations despite available space.

- **Ryan Peterson questioned** why Perry maintenance budget decreased $10,000 each of last two years and where $500,000+ city allocation went.

- **Rory Peterson noted** vote timing: "No debate concerning Perry's fate by any candidates prior to recent 2025 board elections, so voters were deprived of their voice."

- **Applause and cheering followed nearly every speaker**; multiple standing ovations.

- **Board members remained silent throughout**; no questions asked during 2-hour public comment period.

- **Multiple references to Board Member Holly Houk's presence during 2013 Kishwaukee closure**: "Holly, we know," prompting visible reaction.
