## The lede

- The committee approved the county's fiscal year 2027 budget and property tax levy, which came in with a lower-than-expected insurance increase and a projected $200,000-$250,000 general fund surplus for the current year.
- Members approved a $109,978 heavy-duty truck purchase for the highway department using state bid pricing that came in lower than expected due to delayed buying.
- The committee approved lawn maintenance and snow removal contracts with the current vendor, Lawn Maintenance Service, despite some components costing more than competitors.
- County Treasurer Newport reported his office is operating with two staff members instead of three following his chief deputy's October retirement and asked for patience as hiring continues.

## Attendance & housekeeping

Chair Wahlberg called the meeting to order at 6:02 p.m. Present: Carl Johnson, Josh Shoemaker, David Cordray, Brian Schneider, David Wiltse, Lisa Patterson, Tom Wahlberg, Matt Ragano, Dana Logston, and Darian Thornberry. The committee voted to allow Mr. Riley to attend remotely. The committee approved minutes from the October 8 meeting and the agenda as presented. No public attended for public comment.

## Decisions & votes

**October 8 meeting minutes** — Approved unanimously with no discussion.

**Heavy duty truck bid** — Approved unanimously to purchase a 2027 F-550 chassis from Bob Ridings for $109,978 to replace a 1988 truck with 140,000 miles. Highway Engineer Kron explained the department delayed the budgeted purchase after hearing 2027 chassis prices would drop from $56,000 to $53,000. The truck will be used for patching, road striping, and shoulder work.

**Bridge construction grant support resolution** — Approved unanimously for Squaw Prairie, Marengo, and Lawrenceville bridges over the Pecatonica Creek. Kron said the county received federal planning and design funding earlier this year and anticipates construction in 2029-2030. The grant application deadline falls before the December board meeting.

**IDOT force main temporary use permit** — Approved unanimously to allow the state to run sewer lines across county property near the animal services access road. Kron explained IDOT's septic system failed at their facility near the county highway department and they lack space for a new leach field. Cordray asked whether water lines should be installed at the same time; Kron agreed it would make sense but said the state capital development board handles procurement. The permit will eventually lead to a permanent easement request.

**Fiscal year 2027 budget and tax levy** — Approved unanimously with no changes from prior drafts. Administrator Tobin said the county expects to finish the current year $200,000-$250,000 under budget in the general fund despite corrections and sheriff overtime running significantly over budget ($333,000 versus $265,000 budgeted for corrections; $345,000 versus $270,000 for sheriff). Revenue continues to exceed projections due largely to inflation-driven sales tax increases. Johnson thanked staff and noted recent budgets have been easier than his early years on the board.

**CIRMA insurance renewal** — Approved unanimously at $1,402,251, a 5.73% increase. Tobin said the county anticipated a 10% increase but the sale of Maple Crest offset construction-related premium increases. Cyber security premiums increased. Medical facility professional liability remained flat. Logston asked whether CIRMA remains cost-effective; Tobin said the county has been a founding member and receives surplus distributions when available, including approximately $60,000 two years ago.

**Public defender salary** — Approved unanimously to set Aaron Busemi's salary at the state-required rate after the 17th Circuit appointed him to the position. The interim public defender will return to his previous salary. Busemi previously served as an assistant public defender for 12-14 years.

**Lawn maintenance and snow removal contracts** — Approved unanimously to award both contracts to current vendor Lawn Maintenance Service, the low bidder overall despite some individual components coming in higher than competitors Langton Group and Andy's. Facilities Manager Lewis said salt prices increased from $73 to $96 per ton. Patterson asked whether the county could negotiate with closer bidders; Tobin said the county generally doesn't after bids open but can adjust service calls, such as skipping mowing weeks without rain. Schneider confirmed a purchased skid loader is designated for McKinley, not courthouse snow removal. Ragano asked about year-over-year cost increases; Tobin said the vendor held prices steady from 2023-2024 and increased only 2% last year.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**Treasurer's report** — Newport reported October was a three-paycheck month with general fund expenses exceeding revenues by $1.7 million. Investment pool interest rates continue to inch up slowly; about one-fourth of the $40 million pool remains liquid in money markets while the rest is invested in CDs and Treasury securities as rates improve. The final property tax distribution of approximately $225,000-$230,000 for the general fund posted the day after the meeting. Newport said his office is operating short-staffed following Chief Deputy Karen Smiley's October retirement and he hopes to extend a job offer by week's end. Johnson asked about candidates; Newport said he's conducted several interviews and has one highly qualified prospect plus other acceptable candidates.

**Monthly financial report** — Tobin reported the county is 11 months into the fiscal year and should be at 91-92% of budget. Revenue continues to outperform projections due largely to inflation-driven sales tax increases. Corrections overtime hit $333,000 against a $265,000 budget; sheriff overtime reached $345,000 against a $270,000 budget. Some offset occurs because vacant positions reduce salary line items. Tobin anticipates finishing $200,000-$250,000 under budget for the year in the general fund. Construction project expenses total approximately $19.5 million with an anticipated $22.5 million total and roughly $3 million in remaining bills. The courthouse should reach substantial completion by mid-January. The public defender moves into new space next Thursday; the state's attorney moves the week before Christmas. Health department and Logan campus construction is nearly complete at $4.3 million with one outstanding bill; roofing finished and heat is restored.

## Notable moments

Before the meeting formally began, members discussed what appeared to be nationwide price increases with an approximate 15% surcharge, though the context was unclear from pre-meeting conversation.

Wahlberg joked he saw "Justin back there chomping at the bit wanting to talk more" when asking for public comment, though no public attended.

When Cordray suggested IDOT and the capital development board might not pursue water line installation because "it makes sense," members laughed.

Johnson told newer board members they are "lucky" to serve during a period when the county isn't "having to sit here and pull our hair out" trying to balance the budget, noting his early years were more difficult.

Tobin said the required county board member training falls on the same night as Boone County's December 7 organizational meeting, with the next-closest session four to five hours away in Adams County. She asked the training organizer about rescheduling or remote options but was told no accommodations would be made despite many counties facing the same conflict.
