## The lede

- **Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) nearing adoption**: Committee discussed final revisions to replace existing zoning and subdivision regulations, with Farm Bureau requesting changes to livestock facility setback requirements to match state statute rather than more restrictive county standards.
- **Livestock facility buffer zones debated**: Key discussion focused on whether to require reverse setbacks (preventing residential development near existing livestock operations) and how to track facility locations for enforcement.
- **Zoning map consolidation approved for discussion**: New map consolidates commercial districts into one category and identifies planned developments with hash marks; will accompany UDO adoption.
- **FY 2027 budget process outlined**: Administration presented budget calendar starting with department reviews in August and presentations in September; Chairman Johnson proposed forming ad hoc budget committee to increase board involvement.

## Attendance & housekeeping

**Present:** Carl Johnson (Chairman), Dave Cordray, Brian Schneider, Dave Wilty, Tom Wahberg (Finance Chair), Matt Reano, Dana Logston, Marian Thornberry, Rodney Riley (arrived 6:15 PM)

**Absent:** Board members Curry, Patterson, and Shoemaker (out of town)

**Meeting called to order:** 6:00 PM by Chairman Johnson. No minutes to approve. Agenda approved unanimously on motion by Wilty, second by Schneider.

## Decisions & votes

**Motion to approve 60-month FMV lease from Marco ($113/month)**
- **Vote:** Unanimous approval to move to County Board
- **Discussion:** Amy Gallo (Probation) explained the copier lease replacement saves $10/month and adds color copying capability. Motion by Wilty, second by Schneider.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**Unified Development Ordinance (TA-UDO 2026)**
- Consultant Jake from Sightland presented updated UDO draft (June 9 version) consolidating zoning and subdivision regulations into user-friendly document
- Farm Bureau (John Mitchell, President) raised concerns about livestock facility setback requirements on page 86:
  - Current draft requires 500 ft from lot lines and 1,320 ft from non-agricultural uses
  - Farm Bureau requested language match state Livestock Management Facilities Act (quarter-mile from occupied dwellings, not lot lines)
  - Requested addition of reverse buffer zones preventing residential development within quarter-mile of existing livestock facilities
- Board discussion centered on enforcement challenges: Jessica Roberts (Planning) noted difficulty tracking livestock facility locations without self-reporting requirement
- Solutions proposed: Request current list from Department of Agriculture; require subdivision applicants to verify no conflicts; add requirement to planned development applications
- State's Attorney to review whether county can be more restrictive than state statute
- Wilty raised concern about 15-foot side setback for animal housing facilities being too close to property lines
- Staff will revise language for next week's County Board meeting incorporating state statute language and planned development provisions

**Zoning Map Amendment (MA-Zoning Map 2026)**
- New map consolidates commercial zones into single district (one color)
- RC and RC1 districts (conservation residential) eliminated as outdated
- Planned developments (including Candlewick) marked with hash marks
- No parcels being rezoned; only reflecting UDO district consolidations
- May 27 version presented; ready to move forward

**FY 2027 Budget Discussion**
- Becky Tobin (Administration) presented budget calendar with department presentations starting September
- Department review meetings scheduled last two weeks of August
- Proposed allowing Finance Chair and select board members to participate in August budget review meetings with department heads
- Chairman Johnson proposed forming ad hoc budget committee (up to 5 members) to increase board involvement in budget process, noting budget oversight is board's primary responsibility
- Wilty expressed interest in serving on proposed committee
- Registration deadline for UCCI annual conference: June 26

**Monthly Financial Reports**
- County halfway through fiscal year; most accounts at approximately 50% of budget
- Draft audit received; final audit presentation scheduled for next Thursday's County Board meeting
- Revenues tracking well overall; watching circuit court fees/fines and county clerk fees (lower than budget)
- Interest revenue lower due to $5M transfer from general fund to capital fund in November (interest went to capital fund instead of general fund)
- Expenditures: Corrections (57% of overtime spent) and Sheriff (over 60% of overtime spent) being monitored closely
- Treasurer's report available on BoardDocs (Treasurer Newport not present)

## Public comment

**John Mitchell, Winnebago-Boone Farm Bureau President** (1925 South Meridian Road, Rockford) submitted letter regarding UDO livestock facility provisions:
- Expressed concern about proposed 500 ft lot line and 1,320 ft non-agricultural use setbacks conflicting with state Livestock Management Facilities Act
- State law requires quarter-mile from occupied dwellings for new/expanded facilities
- Requested language reflect state statute rather than more restrictive county requirements
- Emphasized both large operations and smaller farms expanding could be negatively affected
- Advocated for continuing reverse buffer zones (currently in code) preventing development near existing livestock facilities

## Notable moments

- **Power flicker during presentation**: Lights briefly went out during Tobin's financial report; she joked "we just lost power... just my eyes" to laughter
- **Speedway analogy**: Wahberg compared potential livestock facility conflicts to "Rockford Speedway syndrome" where subdivision residents later complained about noise from pre-existing racetrack
- **Property line stakes debate**: Thornberry raised practical question about costs of surveying lot lines for setback compliance, leading to explanation of why state statute uses occupied structures instead
- **"It's a small county" observation**: Marie (Farm Bureau) noted confidentiality concerns about member information but acknowledged "it's a small county. You pretty much know where the main dairies and beef operations and swine operations" are
- **Livestock facilities "not popping up everywhere"**: Comment noting extensive discussion focused on scenarios unlikely to occur frequently in current agricultural market
- **Chairman's budget philosophy shift**: Johnson reflected that past budget work focused on cuts during lean times, while current challenge is ensuring departments can be effective with available resources
