## The lede
- The Committee of the Whole meeting opened with presentations honoring Belvidere Police officers: five officers received Alliance Against Intoxicated Motorists (AAIM) awards for DUI enforcement in 2025, and Officer Austin Smaha received his official badge after completing his 18-month probationary period.
- More than a dozen residents spoke during public comment opposing a proposed data center project known as "Project Hawkeye," criticizing the city for alleged lack of transparency after 300+ pages of correspondence were released through FOIA requests showing discussions dating back to 2024.
- The council approved agreements with Boone County, Flora Township, and the Illinois Tollway regarding the Irene Road Bridge, which will cost the city approximately $90,000 for recent repairs with payment deferred until the next budget year.
- The council approved $159,011.41 for emergency repairs to two failing disc filter vessels at the Wastewater Treatment Plant, waiving the bidding requirement due to urgency.
- Fire Chief Shawn Schadle presented the department's 2026 annual report showing 3,495 total incidents with call volume up 3.1% and patient transports increasing more than 5% year-over-year.

## Decisions & votes

**Irene Road Bridge - Local Maintenance Agreement**
- **Motion**: Approve Intergovernmental Agreement between Boone County, Flora Township Road District, and the City of Belvidere for maintenance of the Irene Road Bridge
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: The agreement allocates responsibilities among local entities, with the city and county each paying 50% of capital improvements to the bridge deck and above; Flora Township responsible for routine maintenance like plowing and pothole repair. The city's share of recently completed repairs is approximately $90,000.

**Irene Road Bridge - Tollway Agreement**
- **Motion**: Approve Intergovernmental Agreement for the Irene Road Interchange between the Illinois Tollway, City of Belvidere, Boone County, and Flora Township Road District
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: This new agreement replaces the 2013 agreement and reduces future financial liability for local entities by limiting responsibility to the deck and above rather than the entire superstructure.

**Disc Filter Repairs - Waiving Bidding**
- **Motion**: Waive the bidding process for disc filter repairs at the Waste Water Treatment Plant
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: Council discussed the 15-year lifespan of the filters versus the recommended 10-year maintenance interval. The decision was made to repair only the two worst filters (units #2 and #3) now and budget for the other two in future years, despite potential cost savings of doing all four at once ($291,000 vs. $159,011 for two).

**Disc Filter Repairs - Contract Award**
- **Motion**: Approve proposal from Veolia Water Technologies in the amount of $159,011.41 for rebuild of disc filter vessels #2 and #3, paid from Sewer Depreciation Account #61-1790
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: This was not a budgeted item; filters showed significant signs of bending and metal wear requiring immediate repair.

**Kishwaukee Riverfront Path Extension - Design Change Order**
- **Motion**: Approve design engineering change order request from ARC Design Resources in the amount of $17,540 for the Kishwaukee Riverfront Path Extension, increasing total design cost from $96,500 to $114,040, paid from grant funds and capital funds
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: The increase was necessary because structural and electrical subconsultants' original proposals from August 2024 expired due to delays in securing grant funding; new proposals reflect 2026/27 pricing. Construction observation services (estimated at 5-6% of contract amount) will be a separate proposal.

**Allen Street Pond Outlet to River Project - Bid Award**
- **Motion**: Approve low bid from N-Trak Group in the amount of $1,987,322.50 for the Allen Street Pond Outlet to River Project, paid from capital funds
- **Vote**: Approved by voice vote (aye)
- **Discussion**: This is the first phase of the Fifth Avenue storm sewer overflow and reconstruction project; FY27 budget included $5.9 million for the entire project. Two bids were received (N-Trak Group and Fiser Excavating).

## Discussion items

**Data Center Public Opposition**: Fourteen residents spoke against "Project Hawkeye," a proposed data center project on unincorporated land south of I-90 along Genoa Road. Speakers raised concerns about:
- Alleged lack of transparency, citing 300+ pages of correspondence released via FOIA showing discussions dating to 2024
- Environmental impacts on air quality, water supply, aquifers, and wildlife
- Noise pollution from constant humming of data center operations
- Property value decreases
- Impact on agricultural land and way of life
- Concerns about artificial intelligence safety and data center reliability
- Questions about tax incentives, NDAs, environmental impact studies, and who the end user would be
- Process concerns about annexation and rezoning from agricultural to industrial without community input
- Several speakers noted they live in unincorporated Boone County and cannot vote in city elections but would be affected by city decisions

The mayor did not respond substantively during or after public comment. One speaker became emotional and the mayor cautioned against disruptions from the audience.

**Police Department Recognition**: Five officers were recognized for DUI enforcement: Officers Matthew Korn (third AAIM award), Joseph Danielak, Anthony Jones, Matthew Gibson, and Tyler Stanford. The department made 77 DUI arrests in 2025. Officers Korn and Jones also received recognition from Mothers Against Drunk Drivers in Springfield. Deputy Chief Daniel Smaha presented his son, Officer Austin Smaha, with his official badge after completing 18 months probation.

**Fire Department Operations**: Chief Schadle reported the department canceled its annual open house due to insufficient staffing to safely conduct demonstrations including extrication, live fire sprinkler demonstration, and helicopter landing zone (requiring 200-foot protected area). The department responded to 335 calls in the past 30 days (above average), transporting 189 patients. Apparatus issues included Engine 101 out of service for 4 months and Ladder 150 requiring unexpected major engine rebuild (top-end rebuild covered under warranty). A water tank leak on Engine 102 is being managed daily while warranty claim is processed.

**Mutual Aid Imbalance**: Chief Schadle noted mutual aid is becoming lopsided—the department received aid 17 times in the past month versus giving aid 4 times. Over the past year, they received aid 160 times versus giving 54 times. While not currently problematic due to District 2 having two ambulances in city limits and running fewer calls, the chief noted this could require attention in 8-10 years.

**Fire Department Capital Planning**: In response to council questions, Chief Schadle explained an engine purchase is planned for FY29 in the capital budget (currently in FY27). The goal is an 18-year lifespan with engines serving 12 years front-line and 6 years as reserves, creating a staggered 6-year replacement cycle. He is considering stock builds rather than custom to reduce procurement time from two years to under one year and improve value.

**Police Staffing**: Chief Woody reported three officers at Rock Valley Basic Academy, two in field training, one just completed field training and assigned to dayshift, and two open positions with one lateral applicant in polygraph phase.

**Community Policing**: The police department's What's the Point program partnered with Cedar Village neighborhood and the park district for a cookout with K-9 demonstration. Cedar Village presented Deputy Chief Smaha with a $750 check for the K-9 unit.

## Financial / operational notes

- **Tax Levy Timeline**: Budget and Finance Director Sarah Turnipseed announced the estimated tax levy will be presented November 2, 2026. Kevin Kavanagh from Lauterbach & Amen will present police and fire pension actuarial findings at the October 12 meeting. The levy must be filed with the county clerk by the last Tuesday in December.

- **Investment Activity**: The city opened a $2 million two-year CD at 4.45% to lock in favorable rates.

- **City Hall Improvements**: Director Turnipseed is coordinating painting and flooring work and gathering quotes for new council chambers furniture.

- **EMS Revenue**: The fire department has received approximately $547,000 in EMS revenue to date, about 41% through the fiscal year, tracking slightly above target.

- **Fire Department Budget**: FY26 budget was $7,298,961 with actual spending of $7,191,685 (98.53% of budget). Major variances included overtime at 72.72% of budget and equipment at 59.50% of budget.

- **Fire Prevention Activity**: Inspector Lee Revels completed 623 inspections (44% increase over prior year) and published 733 pre-plans (39% increase). The department implemented a permit and plan review process for Emergency Responder Communication Systems to ensure reliable radio communications inside buildings.

- **Fire Training**: Personnel logged 8,482.35 training hours, earned 15 new certifications, and completed 134 recertifications across all 28 members. CPR instructors taught 37 classes to 235 community members.

- **Fire Apparatus Status**: Engine 101 (2023) has 29,456 miles; Engine 102 (2017) has 77,520 miles; Ladder 150 (2011) has 57,769 miles. The department sold Engine 103 in March 2026 for $5,750.

- **Sewer Depreciation Account**: Disc filter repairs will be paid from Account #61-1790.

- **Grant Funding**: Kishwaukee Riverfront Path Extension design costs will be paid from grant funds and capital funds.

- **Storm Sewer Project**: The Allen Street Pond Outlet to River Project is the first phase of the Fifth Avenue storm sewer overflow project; FY27 budget includes $5.9 million for the entire project.

## Public comment

Fourteen speakers addressed the council, all opposing the proposed data center project:

1. **Billy Daringer Spirino** (4420 Genoa Road): A teacher who expressed sadness about lack of transparency regarding the data center, stated corruption starts at the bottom, and urged the council to represent constituents rather than personal interests or wallets.

2. **Michael Novak** (4415 Genoa Road): Opposed the data center, stated residents prefer their current tranquil life without noise, pollution, and traffic over promises of lower tax bills, and challenged the council to tell Datawatt "not here, not in Belvidere."

3. **Lloyd Roberts** (719 Pearl Street): Criticized the mayor for spending 15 minutes responding to public comment (against Robert's Rules of Order), questioned why taxes must continually increase, opposed tax breaks for billion-dollar companies, and stated he will continue attending meetings until council rejects Project Hawkeye.

4. **Ruth Haberstich** (8227 Fruit Farm Road): Read from Aaron Vakervich's account of data center development processes in other states, including claims that developers' attorneys help write ordinances, NDAs are signed, and rezoning occurs without proper impact assessments or public process. Questioned AI reliability and security.

5. **Kay Wilson** (525 North Appleton): Expressed concerns about AI dangers, noted inventors of AI are now calling for it to be slowed down, stated she doesn't want to die because of a data center, and said the data center won't help with taxes despite claims.

6. **William Boughton** (Huba Road, 43-year resident): Questioned why the city planning website's emphasis on preserving farmland was abandoned, asked about NDAs signed by contractors and other parties, questioned who authored environmental impact studies and water line specifications, asked about concessions and tax incentives, questioned recourse for well contamination or property value loss, and asked if the council's mind is already made up.

7. **Katie Boughton** (8505 Huba Road): Lifelong Spring Township resident and daughter of dairy farmers who strongly opposes taking agricultural land out of production, stated she and her husband are invested in the community as retirees and parents of Belvidere High School graduates, and asked the council not to destroy the community.

8. **Anne O'Hare** (8305 Huba Road): Moved from Chicago 16 years ago to escape corrupt politics, found it offensive that the mayor described constituents as wanting to "tar and feather" officials, noted discussions date to 2024, criticized that Spring Township and Boone County were not brought into discussions, compared the situation to cigarette companies initially claiming smoking was safe, and noted the project map shows three phases totaling nearly 800 acres extending to Fernhill Road.

9. **Teresa Stein** (1018 East Lincoln Avenue): Not near the proposed data center site but opposed to it, expressed disbelief that the council could listen to residents' pain without reaction, and stated she would be crying if she had caused such pain.

10. **Jeff Hulsey** (7272 Huba Road): Reviewed the 323 pages of released documents, noted much was about reimbursement agreements and scheduling emails without substance, stated six large buildings would be built 1,000 feet from his property, asked when the council would provide pros and cons, and requested to see Zoom call contents and attachments rather than just email chains.

11. **Rebecca Meyer** (3097 Garden Prairie Road): Asked why the council continuously ignores requests for information and transparency, questioned why she received a large FOIA response after being assured nothing was happening September 8th, asked where research shows positive environmental effects, stated she wants her kids to have the same agricultural community she had, questioned why community engagement wasn't listed until 10 months into Datawatt's timeline, and stated she hasn't been sleeping well. Meyer became emotional during her comments.

12. **Lisa Fitzgerald** (2913 Carlson Road): Dairy farmer who moved to Boone County in 1990, lost her share farm when owners sold in 2018 forcing relocation, now owns property and thought they wouldn't have to move again, hosts farm breakfasts serving 300 people promoting agriculture, explained livestock are sensitive to electricity and can die from electrical issues or inability to be milked, questioned whether noise from a data center outside council members' homes would result in arrest for nuisance, and urged the council to look at facts before deciding residents' lives.

Following public comment, the mayor stated there would be no response and that individuals can comment without having facts. An audience member challenged the mayor for appearing to smirk and roll eyes at a crying speaker; the mayor stated there is no exchange during public comment and directed the meeting to continue.

## Notable moments

- **Emotional Public Comment**: Rebecca Meyer became visibly emotional while speaking, stating she hasn't been sleeping well since learning about the data center project and questioning how the council can sleep at night. An audience member called out the mayor for appearing to smirk or roll eyes while Meyer was crying, leading to a tense exchange where the mayor reiterated that public comment does not involve back-and-forth and people can comment without having facts.

- **Father-Son Badge Presentation**: Deputy Chief Daniel Smaha presented his son, Officer Austin Smaha, with his official police badge after completing 18 months probation. Deputy Chief Smaha noted that 31 years ago he had a photo of himself holding baby Austin at his own academy graduation, and now Austin has a similar photo holding his own son Kaden at his graduation—"when you circle back on life 31 years ago."

- **Open House Cancelled**: Fire Chief Schadle announced the department had to cancel its annual open house because insufficient firefighters were available to safely staff demonstrations including extrication (requiring 3-4 people), live fire sprinkler demonstration (requiring 3+ people for suppression), and helicopter landing zone (requiring 5-6 people for expanded 200-foot protected area).

- **Public Comment Procedural Dispute**: Lloyd Roberts cited Robert's Rules of Order to argue the mayor should not have spent 15 and 13 minutes in previous meetings responding substantively to public comments, as the chair's role should be procedural rather than participatory. The mayor did not respond to this criticism.

- **Agricultural Heritage**: Multiple speakers emphasized their agricultural roots and way of life. Lisa Fitzgerald described hosting farm breakfasts for 300 people and teaching children to milk cows. Katie Boughton identified as the daughter of dairy and row crop farmers. Billy Daringer Spirino spoke of neighbors who "borrow a cup of sugar across the street" and help with farm animals.

- **Transparency Criticism**: Multiple speakers referenced 300+ pages of documents released through FOIA requests showing city discussions with Datawatt Energy dating to 2024, contrasting this with the mayor's September 8th statement that he knew nothing about the project. Jeff Hulsey characterized much of the released material as "jibberjabber" consisting of scheduling emails without substantive content, and requested access to Zoom call recordings and email attachments
