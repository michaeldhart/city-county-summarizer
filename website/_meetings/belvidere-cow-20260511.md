## The lede
- City Council approved reappointments to Planning & Zoning Commission and Historic Preservation Commission with five-year and three-year terms respectively
- Council passed a resolution opposing state legislation that would strip local zoning and land-use authority, favoring municipal control over housing decisions
- Public Works approved three equipment purchases (wheeled excavator, mini excavator) and material testing services; wells #5 and #7 repairs progressing
- Council approved funding application pursuit for FRA Railroad Crossing Elimination Program grant for Appleton Road grade separation project
- Mayor issued National Police Week proclamation honoring law enforcement

## Decisions & votes
- **Reappointment of Daniel Druckrey** to Planning & Zoning Commission (five-year term ending 2031): Approved unanimously
- **Reappointment of Arthur Hyland** to Planning & Zoning Commission (five-year term ending 2031): Approved unanimously
- **Reappointment of Kimberly Coniglio** to Historic Preservation Commission (three-year term ending 2029): Approved unanimously
- **Reappointment of Sonya Dobberfuhl** to Historic Preservation Commission (three-year term ending 2029): Approved unanimously
- **Resolution in Support of Municipal Housing Authority**: Approved unanimously (opposes state "Build Act" legislation)
- **Hampton, Lenzini & Renwick grant application services** (not to exceed $3,600 for FRA Railroad Crossing Elimination Program application): Approved unanimously
- **Testing Service Corporation contract** ($22,750 for Parking Lot #7 material testing): Approved unanimously
- **Purchase of 2026 John Deere 190G excavator** from West Side Tractor Sales ($345,532.85, includes trade-in): Approved unanimously
- **Purchase of 2026 John Deere 60P mini excavator** from West Side Tractor Sales ($140,446.49): Approved unanimously
- **Executive session** pursuant to Section 2(c)(1) to discuss appointment/employment/compensation: Approved 9–0

## Discussion items
- **State housing legislation opposition**: Mayor Morris extensively criticized proposed "Build Act" legislation that would override local zoning authority, arguing it ignores community-specific needs and removes accountability. Alderman Peterson cited State Senator Don DeWitt's similar concerns and alternative Senate Bill 4200. Alderman Albertini raised concerns about lobbyist influence.
- **Water and sewer rate survey**: Director Anderson reported Belvidere ranks last (best) in regional water/sewer rates; even with proposed increase to $52.62/month, city would remain lowest-cost provider
- **Well system updates**: Well #5 back online after ~$115,000 repair; Well #7 expected operational by following Monday after ~$200,000+ repair (including unexpected wire replacement); Well #11 construction on track for November 2026 completion
- **Appleton Road grade separation project**: Discussed in context of Stellantis reopening and increased freight traffic; phased funding approach for engineering then construction
- **Heritage Days planning**: Planning Director Del Rose reported progress on event coordination, Facebook page access restored
- **Business development activity**: Del Rose reported multiple retail/restaurant prospects including Dutch Bros., downtown coffee shop near Logan Avenue, Baskin-Robbins acquiring KFC location

## Financial / operational notes
- **Building permits (April 2026)**: 176 permits totaling ~$3.3 million in construction value (166 residential, 10 commercial); two enterprise zone discounts totaling ~$8,000
- **Well repair costs**: Well #5 repair ~$115,000; Well #7 repair exceeds $200,000 (including unanticipated wire replacement at $15,000–$20,000)
- **Stellantis impact projection**: If operations resume, Director Anderson anticipates ~20% increase in water/sewer revenues; Walmart distribution center could add another ~10% with projected 900 employees
- **Equipment purchases funded from capital**: Wheeled excavator ($345,532.85) and mini excavator ($140,446.49) both from FY2027 Street Department budget allocations ($350,000 and $150,000 respectively)
- **Sourcewell cooperative purchasing**: Mini excavator secured at 27% discount off list price; wheeled excavator list price $452,454, purchased for $345,532.85 (includes trade-in)
- **Grant application cost**: Hampton, Lenzini & Renwick $3,600 for FRA grant application preparation (paid from line item 41-5-110-8025)
- **Material testing**: $22,750 contract with Testing Service Corporation for Parking Lot #7 project (federal and IDOT funding requires enhanced inspection/reporting); paid from grant and capital funds
- **Historic Preservation grants**: Commission awarded two property maintenance grants totaling $3,000 for doors and windows
- **Zonta fundraiser**: Successfully raised funds for four college scholarships for county students

## Notable moments
- **National Police Week proclamation**: Mayor Morris read proclamation honoring police officers past and present, presented to Police Chief Woody; emphasized department's vital role in community safety
- **Mayor's extended remarks on housing legislation**: Morris delivered passionate critique of state Build Act, warning it would "destroy any decision-making that affects the quality of life for every resident"; referenced failed public housing projects like Cabrini-Green and Robert Taylor Homes as cautionary examples of state overreach
- **Bipartisan local control stance**: Mayor explicitly stated opposition to Build Act transcends party lines, emphasizing protection of local authority
- **Equipment replacement timeline**: City replacing 1999 Komatsu excavator and 2004 Vermeer mini excavator, highlighting multi-decade equipment lifecycles
- **Zonta event appreciation**: Mayor publicly thanked Planning Director Del Rose for organizing successful scholarship fundraiser involving both police and fire chiefs
