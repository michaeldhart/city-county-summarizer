## The lede

- The Committee of the Whole received actuarial presentations for both the police and fire pension funds, with recommended 2026 contributions of $2,208,498 for police (up 0.48%) and $1,705,540 for fire (up 3.05%).
- The board approved $121,226 in construction services for the Allen Street Pond Outlet to River Project and awarded a $244,500 contract to Path Construction for Main Street Bridge expansion joint repairs.
- Two residents spoke against a proposed data center project during public comment, with one citing a nearly 3,000-signature online petition in opposition.

## Decisions & votes

**Allen Street Pond Outlet Project Construction Services** (Item 4B)  
Approved a proposal from CES, Inc. for construction services not to exceed $121,226 (6.1% of the $1,987,322.50 construction contract). Work to be paid from capital funds. Project expected to start fall 2026 and complete by spring 2027.  
Vote: 8-0 (Albertini, Frank, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, Montalbano, Peterson, Stevens in favor)

**Main Street Bridge Resolution** (Item 4C)  
Forwarded to City Council a Resolution for Improvement Under the Illinois Highway Code appropriating $244,500 in Motor Fuel Tax funds for Main Street Bridge expansion joint replacement.  
Vote: Voice vote, carried unanimously

**Main Street Bridge Construction Contract** (Item 4D)  
Awarded contract to Path Construction Company, Inc. for $244,500 (low bid among five bidders ranging from $244,500 to $770,684). Work to be paid from Motor Fuel Tax funds.  
Vote: Voice vote, carried unanimously

**Main Street Bridge Construction Engineering** (Item 4E)  
Approved agreement with Hampton, Lenzini & Renwick, Inc. for construction engineering services not to exceed $22,560. Work to be paid from Motor Fuel Tax funds.  
Vote: Voice vote, carried unanimously

**K9 Unit Donation** (Item 6A)  
Accepted $750 donation from Townhomes of Cedar Village for purchase of K9 unit supplies and equipment.  
Vote: Voice vote, carried unanimously

## Discussion items

**Police and Fire Pension Funding**  
Kevin Cavanaugh from Lauterbach & Amen presented actuarial valuations. The fire fund showed 63.87% funded status, while police showed 68% funded. Both funds had strong investment returns for the year—20.7% for fire and 22.47% for police, well above the expected 6.75% rate. The city has contributed 100% of recommended amounts for the past five years for both funds. One firefighter out of 28 is currently retirement-eligible, while seven out of 41 police officers have met retirement criteria.

**Main Street Bridge Project Details**  
The bridge will remain open during construction with possible one-lane restrictions. Work involves only expansion joint replacements at two locations (Sta. 104+84.50 and Sta. 107+12.50) and immediately adjacent concrete. The deficiency was identified in the bridge's 2022 condition report; temporary repairs were made in 2024. The bridge receives biannual inspections.

## Financial / operational notes

The building department issued $69,427 in commercial and residential permits during September. Code enforcement had 15 violations in progress, conducted four forced mows, and closed seven cases. The department recently interviewed candidates for a vacant position.

The planning department has two cases scheduled for October public hearing: a variance for a property on Marigold and a special use permit for a daycare center with a rentable indoor play area.

Public Works completed branch pickup on the south side of town and began on the northwest side. The 2026 leaf collection program will start October 26 on the north side, alternating weekly between north and south sides through November 16.

## Public comment

Ruth Haberstich of 8227 Fruit Farm Road spoke against data centers, citing congressional investigations and legislative efforts regarding AI data center infrastructure costs. She referenced Amazon's $200 billion projected spending on data centers, Google's $175-185 billion, Meta's $115-135 billion, and Microsoft's $100-120 billion. She cited a pending ComEd rate increase request of $234.3 million for capital costs driven by grid upgrades for data centers and electric vehicle infrastructure, on top of a $243 million rate hike approved in December 2025.

Marie Novak of 4415 Genoa Road opposed a reimbursement agreement between the city and DataWatt Energy related to "Project Hawkeye," requesting transparency on how funds would be used for "miscellaneous city out-of-pocket expenses." She noted an online petition against the project had gathered 2,968 signatures as of 5:00 p.m. that day. She said attendance was lower than previous meetings because of a conflicting meet-the-candidates event.

## Notable moments

Cavanaugh highlighted that investment returns for both pension funds during the year were nearly three times the combined employee and city contributions, demonstrating the value of long-term pension fund investment growth.

Community Development Planner Gina DelRose joked about "getting a raise and promotion" after being referred to as "Director" multiple times during the meeting.

Aldermen Brereton and Fleury were absent from the meeting.

The board did not enter executive session, though it had been listed on the agenda for discussion of collective bargaining matters.
