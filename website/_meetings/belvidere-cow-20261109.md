## The lede

- The Committee of the Whole approved forwarding three tax levy ordinances to City Council that maintain current tax rates with no increase for the fifth time in six years
- The committee postponed a decision on installing stop signs at East 6th Street and Persons Parkway near Meehan Elementary School for one month to explore moving a traffic signal to that location instead
- Officer Ethan Shook received his official Belvidere Police Department badge (number 435) after completing 18 months of probation and training
- More than 60 residents continued their campaign against the proposed Project Hawk Eye data center, with citizens apologizing for recent threats made against an alderman while requesting the project be banned entirely

## Decisions & votes

**Tax levy ordinances (all forwarded to City Council):**
- Corporate purposes levy (flat-line, no increase): Passed 6-1 (Hoiness voted no)
- Special Service Area #2 levy: Passed unanimously by voice vote
- Special Service Area #3 levy: Passed unanimously by voice vote

**Development application fees ordinance:** Passed unanimously by voice vote. Creates new "plat of consolidation" category for small parcel combinations at half the cost of typical final plat fees ($300 commercial, $250 residential).

**Sale of surplus vehicles and equipment:** Passed unanimously by voice vote. Authorizes sale of seven items including tractors, trucks, and saws from 1976 to 2004.

**IDOT sewer connection request at 9797 Route 76:** Forwarded to City Council by voice vote (passed with no opposition recorded). IDOT facility seeks to replace failing septic system by connecting to city sanitary sewer.

**Jeff Peters appointment to Ida Public Library Board:** Passed unanimously by voice vote. Fills unexpired term through June 30, 2027.

**Generac generator donation:** Passed unanimously by voice vote. Fire Department will receive IQ5200 portable generator valued at approximately $1,000 for use with smoke safety trailer at schools.

**Health insurance renewal:** Committee waived referral requirement under section 2-88 (passed unanimously) and forwarded to next week's City Council meeting (passed unanimously). Initial quote showed 41% premium increase; final proposals to be presented next week.

**East 6th Street and Persons Parkway intersection:** Motion to install three-way stop was postponed for one month (passed unanimously) to explore relocating traffic signal from north of intersection to the south side when IDOT improves Belvidere Road.

**Executive session:** Entered executive session 7-0 to discuss collective bargaining and personnel matters.

## Discussion items

**Intersection safety at East 6th and Persons Parkway:** School District 100 requested stop signs on East 6th Street citing safety concerns for students crossing to Meehan Elementary. Director Keck's review found the intersection does not meet IDOT Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices warrants: only one accident in five years, East 6th carries 2,200 average daily traffic versus 200 or less on Persons Parkway. Concerns raised about creating bottleneck and backing up traffic to nearby Jim Gang Highway. Discussion focused on IDOT plan to relocate existing traffic signal to the intersection when Belvidere Road is improved.

**Project Hawk Eye data center opposition:** Nine residents spoke against the proposed data center. Topics included: corporate tactics used to sell communities on data centers, lack of local jobs from construction (contractors from outside area), noise pollution from diesel generators, environmental concerns, property value impacts, comparison to 43 of 44 communities voting to ban data centers in recent elections, Elon Musk's shift to satellite-based data centers, and technology not proven at proposed scale. Residents apologized for threats made against an alderman while maintaining peaceful opposition. One speaker questioned why discussions continue when aldermen appear opposed and mayor cannot unilaterally approve the project.

**UW Health expansion:** Director Del Rose reported UW Health is under contract to purchase seven parcels on South State Street with discussions on two additional properties. Preliminary plans include vacating portions of unused Garfield and Howland streets and potentially building a clinic to move non-emergency services out of the hospital. Project timeline estimated at a couple years; no applications received yet.

**Annexation policy:** Discussion of requiring annexation agreements for properties receiving city water and sewer outside city limits, referencing 20-year Deer Hills agreement that ended earlier in 2026 where properties paid no city taxes while receiving services.

## Financial / operational notes

**Building Department revenues:** October permits totaled $65,346.37. Code enforcement closed 10 cases, has 11 in progress, and one forced mowing.

**Public Works equipment:** New wheeled excavator purchased; 1999 Komatsu broke down before trade-in and is being repaired. City still negotiating potential trade with West Side Tractors.

**East Gate facility:** Permits issued for gate work, paving, and roof repairs at the 5.4 million square foot building. Test track may be installed.

**Tax levy details:** Corporate purposes levy remains unchanged from prior year at approximately $5.69 million. Does not include Ida Public Library's requested $895,340 amount, which would represent 5% increase. City's portion of total property tax burden approximately 12.5% compared to school district's 67-68%. Mayor noted city has flat-lined levy five out of last six years; only increase was for paramedic transport after Lifeline Ambulance closure.

**Health insurance:** First renewal quote showed 41% premium increase. Brokers negotiated down. Final quotes include Blue Cross Blue Shield (less beneficial plan requiring higher city buy-down) and United Health Care (same plan as current). Open enrollment scheduled for early December.

**IDOT sewer connection:** IDOT Belvidere facility will pay connection fees and ongoing sewer usage fees. No cost to city; facility pays for service line installation. Engineer confirmed adequate capacity in existing 4-inch force main.

**Tax levy public hearing:** First reading scheduled November 16; final reading with public hearing December 7.

## Public comment

Ruth Haberstitch apologized for threats against officials and cited Google AI analysis of how tech companies use "sophisticated corporate playbook" exploiting economic weaknesses, promising jobs that don't materialize, and using NDAs to prevent public organizing.

Billy Sperosino questioned how mayor spent $150,000 without council approval when spending limit is around $15,000, asked who paid for drones and drilling at Nowak's property, and suggested banning data centers altogether with possibility of referendum to lift ban later.

Michael Nowak stated group feels heard by council's actions to block project, condemned threats against officials, and asked for complete end to data centers in Boone County to preserve farmland community.

Mark Final questioned job promises when crane companies and excavation contractors are from outside area (Alsip, Arlington Heights, Wauconda, McHenry), noted lack of local hotels, and suggested building houses instead on five to ten acre lots.

Rebecca Meyer challenged Data Watt's qualifications, noting company has never managed a data center and cannot provide performance data because "a data center of this magnitude simply does not exist yet." Called out Mayor Morris, Alderman Peterson, and Alderman Stevens specifically for supporting "unproven experiment."

Lisa Fitzgerald cited Sunday Today report that 43 of 44 communities voted November 3rd to ban data centers. Noted US has over 5,400 data centers versus China's 400. Questioned accountability citing pilot gas station's unfulfilled hotel promise. Referenced Revelations 11:18 about destroying those who destroy the earth.

Ashley Cummins, resident for four years, stated she has no local ties and is considering selling house due to data center concerns. Questioned why discussions continue if aldermen are opposed and mayor cannot act unilaterally. Noted 75% across party lines oppose data centers, calling it "not political."

Paul McAffrey reported Elon Musk plans to relocate data center resources to satellites (targeting 1,000 satellites initially) due to lower costs, no regulations, solar power, extreme cold eliminating cooling needs, and minimal staffing. Cited Musk's prediction that data centers will close within five years, questioning city's plan for 760-acre "concrete jungle" if facility goes bankrupt.

Final speaker (name not fully captured) compared Mayor Morris to "wannabe king" for taking no salary while publicizing it, strong-arming aldermen, claiming independence while ignoring constituents, and ignoring science. Urged mayor to table data center idea and return money to Data Watt.

## Notable moments

Officer Ethan Shook received his permanent badge after completing two years of testing, background checks, 16-week police academy, five-month field training, and 18-month probationary period. Sergeant Ball explained the rigorous process and thanked family and field training officers. Mayor Morris thanked Shook for choosing law enforcement, calling it "an honorable profession" at a time when it has not received "a fair shake."

Multiple residents apologized for recent threats made against an alderman, emphasizing their opposition has been peaceful and they do not condone such actions.

Alderman Brereton announced he will introduce an ordinance at Monday's council meeting to ban data centers in the city of Belvidere, presenting a draft to the city attorney.

Mayor Morris explained the city portion of property taxes is about 12.5% of total levy, with school district comprising 67-68%. Noted frustration that residents blame the city for tax increases when the city has flat-lined its portion five out of six years.

Alderman Albertini questioned "how do we change that pie?" regarding the disproportionate share going to the school district, saying he doesn't "see why we're giving them more" given how the district is "squandering" money.

Discussion revealed the 2005 Deer Hills agreement allowed properties to receive city water and sewer while paying no city taxes for 20 years, with the city responsible for road repairs. Agreement ended earlier in 2026, just 10 days before it would have continued "maybe forever."

Planning Director Del Rose mentioned she "hate[s] public speaking" after presenting at an APA conference in Evanston.
