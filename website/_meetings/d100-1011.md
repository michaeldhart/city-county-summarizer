## The lede

- The board approved raising the driver's education fee cap from $250 to $400 after holding a public hearing, citing the district's actual program costs exceeding the current limit.
- The district's FY 2026 audit showed a $10.8 million operating deficit with a financial profile score of 3.45, down from 3.55 the previous year, primarily due to deficit spending and long-term debt.
- Special education teachers from Mihan Elementary spoke publicly about unsustainable workloads, lack of planning time, and requests for a fifth resource teacher that have been repeatedly denied.
- The board discussed whether to abate $4 million or $5 million in property taxes using county sales tax revenue, with Business Services Director recommending keeping the levy flat at a $4 million abatement to maintain budget stability.

## Attendance & housekeeping

All seven board members were present: Barbi Yunk, Jermaine Pigee (not present for public comment), Scott Mueller, Holly Houk, Jorge Herrera, Jeff Diestelmeier, and Brian Hoemke.

The meeting began at 5:30 p.m. with closed session, reconvened in open session at 6:29 p.m., and included presentations from Belvidere High School Choraliers before formal business began.

## Decisions & votes

**Agenda approval** (Approved unanimously)  
Hoemke moved, Houk seconded. No discussion.

**Consent agenda items 1-12** (Approved unanimously)  
Including minutes, personnel report, financial reports, bills, MOU with Boone County Health Department, excess property disposal, facility use requests, overnight field trips (DECA to Anaheim and Rosemont), fundraiser requests, and donations totaling over $1,200 from First United Methodist Church, Boone County Arts Council, IOU Club, and Northern Illinois Building Contractors Association.  
Houk moved, Hoemke seconded.

**Policy 7:30 Student Transfers revisions** (Approved unanimously)  
Mueller moved, Diestelmeier seconded. The revised policy clarifies transfer criteria with five specific factors, requires annual approval, and addresses IHSA athletic eligibility limitations. Mr. Mandera explained the changes were prompted by registration concerns and board attention to transfers at the beginning of the school year.

**High school course catalog revisions for 2027-28** (Approved unanimously)  
Hoemke moved, Mueller seconded. No discussion.

**Driver's education fee waiver request** (Approved unanimously)  
Diestelmeier moved, Mueller seconded. The resolution requests a waiver to increase the driver's ed fee from $250 to $400. Dr. Carson explained the district currently charges $275 (already requiring a waiver) and costs exceed $600 per student. Direct-certified families remain exempt from fees. Board members confirmed the district rate would still be lower than private providers.

**Elementary interventionist/ESL workload plan** (Approved unanimously)  
Hoemke moved, Mueller seconded. Dr. Johnson explained the plan provides a formal process when workload concerns arise, showing step-by-step procedures. Mr. Mandera noted the plan has existed informally for three years and is reviewed annually with the BEA.

**Case management/workload plan for special education** (Approved unanimously)  
Mueller moved, Diestelmeier seconded. Extensive discussion followed public comments from Mihan special education teachers. Mrs. Babinec explained the process requires a formal written submission with data before central office intervention. She noted meetings have occurred at Mihan since September addressing scheduling. Board members questioned whether supervisors could sub in classrooms to understand needs firsthand. Dr. Schug noted administrators already sub for principals and assistant principals regularly, and breakthrough coach training has directors in classrooms weekly (Dr. Carson spends Thursdays in all five secondary buildings). Discussion covered special education referral timelines (50 school days vs. the 60-day legal requirement), staffing projections across buildings, and the difference between case load numbers and individual student needs. The standard is approaching 20 students per resource teacher before additional allocation is considered.

**BESSA MOA vacation payout request** (Approved unanimously)  
Hoemke moved, Houk seconded. No discussion.

**Administrative handbook revisions** (Approved unanimously)  
Yunk moved, Mueller seconded. No discussion.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**FY 2026 financial audit presentation**  
Corey from Gorens & Associates presented remotely. The audit showed operating fund revenues of $132.9 million vs. expenditures of $144.6 million. The financial profile score dropped to 3.45 (from 3.55 last year and 3.9 prior), driven by deficit spending, long-term debt, and days cash on hand falling below 180 days to 155 days. Real estate taxes increased $3.5 million to $56.4 million. Federal sources decreased $6 million (primarily ESSER funding ending). Transportation saw a $1 million shortfall due to state proration and deduction of Preschool for All revenue. The district came in at 3% administrative costs, remaining in the fourth (lowest) quartile statewide, requiring no waiver. Salaries and benefits comprise 73% of expenditures. The assessed valuation continues rising while tax rates decline.

**2026 tax levy and sales tax update (first reading)**  
Mrs. Armstrong presented preliminary levy worksheets showing a potential tax rate of 4.5014% with a $4 million abatement (down from 4.764%) or 4.4369% with a $5 million abatement. The district has received nearly $5 million in county sales tax revenue this calendar year. Historical data shows the district abated more than it received from 2018-2021, recently reaching break-even. Armstrong explained any sales tax revenue over $4 million could be moved to Fund 60 (facilities) to offset capital project costs currently in Fund 20 (operations), potentially reducing the operating deficit by up to $1 million. She cautioned that sales tax is volatile, noting January 2026 revenue jumped from $388,000 to $519,000 compared to January 2025. Discussion addressed whether to maintain the $4 million abatement as planned with Nexus and PTMA or abate the full $5 million. Armstrong stated both approaches are valid but recommended keeping the levy flat as communicated to the public, while respecting the original agreement to abate what is received. The board will discuss further at the December 1 Business Services Committee meeting before the December 14 adoption hearing. Armstrong clarified the $10.8 million operational deficit excludes Funds 30, 60, and 90, which intentionally deficit-spend as they draw down bond proceeds and transferred reserves.

**IASB Delegate Assembly resolutions**  
Dr. Schug reported board representative Jermaine Pigee reviewed all resolutions and plans to vote as recommended in the attached report. Topics include pension reform (Tier 2 funding), FOIA reform addressing AI-generated requests, and other straightforward items. Board members expressed comfort with Pigee's planned votes.

## Public comment

**Jennifer Shepard, Mihan Elementary resource teacher**, described working beyond capacity with potentially no daily plan time, down from 25 minutes. She has gone without plan time much of the past two years. Her team repeatedly requested a fifth resource teacher but received only alternative schedules and suggestions for larger groups. She cited a math group of six students (one first grader, five second graders) with vastly different skill levels, making meaningful individualized instruction impossible. Paraprofessionals cannot replace certified teacher instruction. Mihan is the only elementary school with a "developing" state designation, partly due to special education students' lack of growth on state assessments. She asked the board to look beyond case load numbers to actual student needs and advocate for additional staffing, warning of teacher burnout.

**Anna Veruchi, Mihan Elementary resource teacher**, reported she has 12 students across four grade levels, collaborates with nine classroom teachers, is on overload pay, and has only 20 minutes of prep daily. Three of Mihan's four resource teachers are on overload, serving 50 students total. The school received 13 transfer students this year (10 were overflows from schools with full case loads), added three more students, and has five pending evaluations. Two students are transitioning from the ABC program onto resource case loads. Over the past four years, Mihan averaged 13 initial evaluations and 15 transfer students with existing IEPs annually. She stated schedules are beyond capacity and cannot accommodate more students while providing required service minutes. The principal has advocated for a fifth resource teacher for two years without success. She expressed concern about potential reduction-in-force impacts and asked for a fifth resource teacher, protected planning time, reasonable group sizes, and staffing decisions reflecting actual enrollment and student needs.

**Matt Zickgraf** questioned whether the board was informed about deficit spending before approving new positions including directors and HVAC jobs totaling nearly $40 million over three years, with operating budgets increasing $50 million over six years. He urged prioritizing cuts to things that don't touch kids over those that do. He cited public sector rankings showing District 100 in the bottom 50% of Illinois schools (out of 860) for math and reading proficiency, well below state averages, and noted the district's rate of improvement is also below the state average. He stated alternative assessment scores are five times below the state average and called for accountability given the half-million dollars paid to two administrators, suggesting the board examine past history before extending contracts.

**A parent (not identified by name)** questioned spending priorities, citing approval of a $55,000 parking lot seal coating bid with $5,500 to Nexus (13.75% vs. the 10% contract rate) for a central office project with no student benefit. She noted Nexus acknowledged the estimate was high but asked if the district gets refunds when projects come under budget. She described a year-long wait to address a classroom door security concern at Washington Academy, ultimately resolved with a half-glass, half-vent door from the office remodel. She asked who approved that as secure and whether the board is involved in repairs. She reported personally contacting the city about a Washington Academy sidewalk trip hazard and learning about a 50% cost-share program, asking why she is finding solutions. She contrasted these safety issues with continued central office project approvals during a $28 million deficit, stating board members should know about problems before she exposes them publicly and warning that failure to address these priorities will result in seat replacements.

**Todd Taylor, BEA president**, wished the board happy Board Appreciation Day and presented handmade coasters from Belvidere North's manufacturing club students. He stated the BEA is willing to explore creative and difficult budget solutions but emphasized thinking beyond next year's budget. He noted significant numbers of experienced educators approaching retirement over the next 3-5 years, which could help address financial challenges through planning and attrition. He asked the board not to treat new teachers as disposable simply because they have fewer service years, warning the district will need them when veterans retire. He said decisions based solely on immediate savings may create future problems and urged examining how the district got here, questioning spending, and ensuring decisions are based on sound information. He stated the BEA's responsibility extends beyond the next budget cycle to ensuring the district has experienced educators and promising new teachers five years from now.

**Emily O'Reilly, Belvidere High School EL teacher**, reported EL/DL teachers have worked relentlessly to support student success, increasing the EL graduation rate from 60% (on state watch list in January 2023) to 86.5% for 2025-26. She stated these gains are jeopardized by elimination of extended algebra classes. Two years ago, extended algebra part A and B provided self-contained EL math with Spanish support as a two-year course for newcomers with interrupted education or below-grade skills. As of fall 2027, this class will no longer exist. The traditional EL algebra self-contained course was also eliminated and converted to push-in. She noted the math curriculum changed this year, already challenging for English-speaking students, and removing Spanish-supported EL algebra is negatively impacting students. Several newcomers are in danger of not earning math credit. Math teachers brought concerns to the EL department. The push-in teacher is exhausted trying to meet individual needs of 10 students lacking skills for the current class structure. She stated they received no clear answer for the change, especially since Belvidere North (with lower EL population) offers self-contained EL algebra. She asked to protect sheltered EL classes, noting push-in models are not always in students' best interests.

Later in the meeting, Mrs. Armstrong's computer experienced problems ("died a heart attack") just as she was beginning her tax levy presentation.

**Fund accounting clarification**: Mrs. Armstrong took time to clarify public confusion about the district's deficit, emphasizing that Funds 30, 60, and 90 intentionally deficit-spend as they draw down bond proceeds and transferred reserves, and should not be included in operational deficit calculations. The actual operational deficit is $10.8 million across operating funds, not the larger figure that includes capital and debt service funds.

**Special education workload discussion length**: The discussion following public comments from Mihan special education teachers and during consideration of the case management workload plan extended significantly, with board members pressing administrators on processes, building-level versus district-level solutions, timeline for evaluations, and whether central office staff understand classroom realities firsthand. Mrs. Babinec and Dr. Schug detailed existing processes, superintendent site visits, breakthrough coach training schedules, and administrator subbing for principals, but board members expressed concern that teachers appearing at board meetings signals exhaustion of other remedies.
