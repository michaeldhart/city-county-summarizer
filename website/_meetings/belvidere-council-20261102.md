## The lede

- The City Council voted 8-2 to adopt a resolution directing the mayor and city administration to cease all negotiations with Datawatt Energy regarding Project Hawkeye, a proposed 3-gigawatt data center campus on Genoa Road.
- More than 45 minutes of public comment featured residents opposing the data center, citing concerns about water use, noise, property values, environmental impact, and the unprecedented scale of the proposed facility.
- The council approved an estimated $6.6 million tax levy for 2026, representing no increase for city operations and a 5% increase for the Ida Public Library.
- Mayor Clinton Morris was absent due to scheduled minor surgery; the meeting was led by the acting presiding officer.

## Decisions & votes

**Resolution 2026-24 (directing cessation of Datawatt Energy negotiations):** Passed 8-2 after amendment. Albertini, Brereton, Fleury, Frank, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, and Montalbano voted yes; Peterson and Stevens voted no. The resolution was amended to insert "also known as" between "data center" and "Project Hawkeye" to make the language more encompassing.

**Estimated tax levy for 2026:** Approved 10-0. The levy totals $6,625,027 ($5,729,687 for city operations with no increase from current levy; $895,340 for Ida Public Library representing a 5% increase).

**October 19, 2026 meeting minutes:** Approved by voice vote.

**October 26, 2026 Committee of the Whole minutes:** Approved by voice vote.

**Insurance renewal with IML RMA:** Approved 10-0. The city will renew property, casualty, and workers' compensation coverage for January 1, 2027 through January 1, 2028 for $805,170.17 (including $2,000 in IML dues), paid in full by November 13, 2026 to receive a 1% discount.

## Discussion items

City Attorney Mike Drella provided extensive analysis on the legal enforceability of the resolution directing the mayor to cease negotiations. He explained that while the council can legally pass such a resolution, it may not be constitutionally enforceable as it could infringe on the mayor's executive authority. Drella clarified that the mayor cannot bind the city to any agreement without council approval, cannot annex land without council vote, and cannot spend money above his $15,000 discretionary limit without council authorization. He noted the resolution serves primarily as a declaration of the council's position.

Alderwoman Frank questioned whether the resolution could expose the city to legal liability. Drella responded that a lawsuit from Datawatt is unlikely to succeed because the property is not within city limits and applicants have no vested right to annexation.

Alderman Albertini submitted a petition with 824 signatures from Boone County registered voters opposing the data center, including 555 signatures from Belvidere city residents.

Discussion addressed whether the resolution would actually prevent Datawatt from holding a public forum, with Drella noting that the company's October 29 letter indicated they plan to hold such a forum regardless of council action.

Alderman Brereton raised concern that the resolution as drafted by the city attorney did not match his original motion verbatim, specifically missing the phrase "also known as" before "Project Hawkeye." He also referenced hearing the project might now be called "Project Carbide."

## Financial / operational notes

The estimated 2026 tax levy represents no increase for city operations compared to the current levy. The Ida Public Library portion represents a 5% increase. The final levy will be discussed at the November 9, 2026 committee meeting before formal adoption.

The insurance renewal represents only a 1% increase from the prior year. Budget and Finance Officer Sarah Turnipseed noted the work involved in obtaining quotes for the coverage.

An agreement signed by Mayor Morris allows Datawatt Energy to place $150,000 in escrow to cover potential city consultant costs if the project moved forward. The agreement creates no liability for the city. Any unused funds would be returned to Datawatt.

Datawatt provided preliminary property tax estimates showing potential revenue to various taxing bodies, but Drella stated he does not believe the numbers and that independent verification would be needed.

## Public comment

Fifteen residents spoke during the 45-minute public comment period, with speakers from the prior week who did not get to speak going first. All speakers opposed the data center project.

Leanne Newman (8669 Centaur Drive) spoke about her family's multi-generational farming history and her purchase of 19.33 acres, expressing concern about the project's impact on Spring Township's peaceful atmosphere.

Cedric Peterson (408 East Locust Street) invoked religious principles, asking whether council members would favor the data center if built next to their own homes, and called "annexation without representation or consideration" a deplorable practice.

Adam Newman (4234 Fern Hill Road) discussed farmland loss in Illinois (77,000 acres annually) and the site's role in groundwater recharge, estimating one acre can put 81,000 to 135,000 gallons into the ground yearly. He noted the 760-acre site would be paved over, preventing water percolation and potentially harming Mosquito Creek and the Kishwaukee River watershed.

Norma Estep (3800 Greenspire Court) cited multiple examples of data center violations nationwide including light pollution, noise violations, water depletion, and contamination. She noted that only one 3,000-megawatt data center exists globally (in Upper Burrell, Pennsylvania, operating only five months in its first phase), meaning no facts exist for a facility this size.

Kay Wilson (Appleton Avenue) criticized the mayor for repeatedly saying he needed "more facts" and questioned whether he had signed a non-disclosure agreement.

Ruth Haberstich (8227 Fruit Farm Road) discussed data center problems including energy consumption, water strain, noise pollution, and vulnerability to hacking. She cited an NBC report from October 30, 2026 about tech market crashes related to AI data center costs, noting Intel fell 28% and does not anticipate positive cash flow this year.

William Boughton asked whether the mayor and city attorney could usurp council authority and whether the zoning and planning committee could override the council.

Billy Sperino (4420 Genoa Road) criticized the mayor for calling residents "uneducated" and noted that a data center worker at a prior meeting said none of his 20 employees came from the town where that center was built.

Vivian Penny (436 Andrews Drive, resident since 1968) asked whether the city's ordinance addresses diesel generator noise, emissions, fuel storage, permits required, and environmental impacts.

Joe Russell (8385 Hubert Road) described operating a 16-acre registered American horse farm and explained how data center noise, construction vibrations, and lighting would harm breeding operations, potentially causing stress-related illnesses, gastric ulcers, and panic that could lead to injuries.

Rebecca Meyer (3097 Garden Prairie Road) called out Mayor Morris, Alderman Peterson, and Alderwoman Frank by name, accusing them of failing to listen to constituents. She thanked the eight aldermen who voted for the resolution at committee. She challenged City Attorney Drella's memo citing Article VII of the Illinois Constitution, stating she could not find language addressing mayoral authority infringement.

## Notable moments

Acting presiding officer (not identified by name in transcript) opened the meeting with extensive ground rules for decorum, citing "jeering, speaking loudly, yelled obscenities, and attempts to engage the council in debate" at previous meetings. The officer warned that disruptive behavior would result in removal and trespassing from city hall.

Alderman Brereton criticized a "unilaterally signed contract" by Mayor Morris with Datawatt (the $150,000 escrow agreement), calling it "null and void" and stating "I knew nothing about until it came out of left field."

Alderman Albertini stated that he has not found a single constituent in his ward who supports the data center and that he voted to represent their wishes, not for political reasons.

The audience applauded multiple times during public comment and after key statements by council members, with the acting presiding officer noting at one point that "at least we know the mics speakers out in the lobby work because they cheered first."

Alderwoman Gramkowski asked whether the project had any name other than "Project Hawkeye" and requested "the truth" about who signed agreements, stating "I'm still not sure that I'm believing people haven't signed agreements."
