## TL;DR

- **Cemetery Road Solar Project approved 7-4** despite strong public opposition; board cited state law stripping local control and threat of lawsuits
- **Huber Road Assembly special use permit sent back to ZBA** (June 23) for further consideration rather than deciding tonight
- **$5.3 million in transportation grants awarded** to Boone County for road projects through R1PC
- **Multiple energy ordinances adopted** establishing regulations and fees for solar, wind, and energy storage systems
- **Major infrastructure spending approved**: $997K bridge replacement, $500K jail cooling system, $257K courthouse masonry work

## Attendance & housekeeping

Meeting called to order at 6:37 p.m. (7 minutes late). Board members Curry and Logston participated electronically with board approval. Roll call showed all members present. Chairman Carl Johnson abstained from certain claims involving his business interests (William Charles, Northern Illinois Service Co., Core & Main, Highway Department).

## Decisions & votes (each with vote count and notable discussion or dissent)

**Appointments approved unanimously** (voice vote):
- Dave Worrell (Board of Review, reappointment)
- Beth Calder (Garden Prairie Street Light District, new) — Chairman specially thanked Calder for filling long-vacant position needed to keep lights operational
- Dan Mason, John Cleland, Cory Lind, Michelle Marella, Rick Schultz (various boards)

**Ordinance 26-21: Cemetery Road Solar Project (SUP-05-2026)** — **APPROVED 7-4**
- Yes: Patterson, Walberg, Riley, Reggano, Johnson, Shoemaker (7)
- No: Cordre, Schneider, Curry, Logston, Thornberry (4)
- 40-acre solar project by Nexamp, reduced from original footprint to 27.5 acres fenced area
- **Significant dissent**: Multiple residents spoke against; board members voting yes expressed they were forced by state law and lawsuit threats
- Riley: "I don't believe in it...I don't want to subject taxpayers to more litigation costs"
- Curry: "I'm always going to vote no. I really don't care about lawsuits"
- Johnson: "We have no choice. State code says if they meet parameters X,Y,Z, you must approve...It will cost this county a ton of money and we will lose"

**Ordinance 26-20: Huber Road Assembly (SUP-04-2026)** — **SENT BACK TO ZBA** (unanimous voice vote)

## Public comment

**Six residents spoke in opposition to Cemetery Road solar project:**

**Randy Watson** (9780 Edson Rd, Capron): Opposed solar on prime ag land; noted 11.4-12% actual capacity vs. 4.95 MW claimed; potato growers refusing to farm former solar land; urged board to "stand up for what is right" and not cave to lawsuit threats.

**Andy Moon** (16340 Mill Rd, Capron): Criticized Nexamp's conduct: lobbying for state law changes, $200K lawsuit, deposing/disrespecting community members; "That's how you treat neighbors in Chicago. Maybe you should just stay home."

**Henry Syssmick** (16215 Mill Rd, Capron): Brief statement asking board to vote no; represented farmers and 38-year county residents.

**Joe McKenna** (1634 Mill Rd): Simple statement opposing project.

**Timothy Shaw** (16900 Cemetery Rd): 50-year resident ~1,500 ft from proposed site; asked board to vote no.

**Maryanne McKenna Moon** (16340 Mill Rd, Capron): Representing McKenna Farms (adjacent property, 50 years); project conflicts with Comprehensive Plan objective 3.7 (protect prime farmland); revisions don't change 40-acre displacement for 45 years with "little or no benefit"; urged board to "stand strong" like Winnebago County and reject project to demonstrate local control should remain with counties.

**Attorney Nathan J. Noble** (504 N State St, Belvidere): Represented Houston Road residents opposing Huber Road Assembly permit; argued ZBA findings of fact not met (health/safety/welfare); cited Spring Township safety concerns, notice violations, open code enforcement, recent party requiring neighbor to block driveway; compared unfavorably to Robas venue (1 house within half-mile vs. 15 for this project); urged board to follow ZBA recommendation and deny.

## Notable moments (anything colorful, tense, or unusual)

**Extended, passionate solar debate**: Chairman Johnson gave detailed explanation of state law stripping county authority: "The state doesn't want you to have a say. They don't care." Noted new legislation would require counties to pay solar companies' attorney fees if they lose lawsuits. Board Member Curry delivered impassioned speech about holding legislators accountable, criticizing glyphosate use, promoting nuclear over solar, and urging constituents to "bring Dave Vella [state rep] in front of everybody" to answer for his vote. **Audience clapped** (against meeting decorum); Johnson admonished: "No clapping please. This isn't a ball game."

**Johnson's blunt assessment**: "We need to make changes [in Springfield]. We're trying to get them to listen, but not necessarily...this particular project meets the parameters...I wish it wasn't [viable]."

**Example of state override**: Chairman noted Rockford spent "hundreds of thousands" on water/sewer infrastructure for I-90 industrial site, now going to solar farm instead because "they can't say no."
