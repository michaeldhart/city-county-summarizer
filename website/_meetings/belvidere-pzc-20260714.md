## TL;DR
- **East Point Commons development** progresses with approvals for rezoning 87 acres to Planned Business and 98 acres to Planned Industrial, plus preliminary and final plat approvals
- Planning Commission **recommended approval** (5-1 votes) for all four East Point Commons cases; one commissioner (Druckrey) consistently voted no over agricultural land loss concerns
- **Public concern raised** about potential data center development and impacts (noise, lighting, traffic) on nearby residential areas; staff clarified strict zoning and landscaping requirements apply
- Development includes 33-lot subdivision with Gateway Center Drive extension and a **1,300-foot cul-de-sac variance granted** (exceeds 500-foot code limit)

## Decisions & votes

**Case 2026-13: East Point Commons Planned Business (MA)**
- Motion to recommend approval carried **5-1** (Druckrey voted no)
- Rezones ~87 acres from RH (Rural Holding) to PB (Planned Business)

**Case 2026-14: East Point Commons Planned Industrial (MA)**
- Motion to recommend approval carried **5-1** (Druckrey voted no)
- Rezones ~98 acres from RH to PI (Planned Industrial)

**Case 2026-15: East Point Commons Subdivision (PP)**
- Motion to recommend preliminary plat approval with 12 conditions carried **5-1** (Druckrey voted no)
- Includes 33-lot subdivision; variance granted for cul-de-sac length exceeding code

**Case 2026-16: East Point Commons Final Plat #1 (FP)**
- Motion to recommend final plat approval with 12 conditions carried **5-1** (Druckrey voted no)
- 6-lot subdivision including industrial lots

**Minutes approval:**
- June 9, 2026 minutes approved **6-0**

## Discussion items

**Comprehensive Plan deviation:** Commission discussed how commercial acreage will approximately triple from what the May 2024 Comprehensive Plan designated (more commercial, less industrial than planned).

**Data center concerns:** Resident Lianne Wojtanek (2753 Huntington Dr.) expressed concern about potential data center development. Staff clarified:
- Data centers typically require 100+ acres (more than available)
- Usually located in industrial zones due to height/vibration requirements
- Would require special use approval if not meeting current code
- Conditional zoning is illegal in Illinois

**Agricultural land loss:** Commissioner Druckrey expressed concern about converting agricultural land; applicant noted development is approximately 50 acres per 20 years.

**Cul-de-sac variance:** Dusty Trail Court at ~1,300 feet exceeds 500-foot code limit; variance justified for industrial access with larger turning radius for trucks.

**Buffering/landscaping:** Staff explained four-category landscaping requirements (building, road frontage, parking lot, miscellaneous) designed to buffer light, noise, and visual impacts on nearby residential areas.

**Zoning standards:** Staff confirmed lighting, vibration, noise, and odor standards apply citywide; planned industrial requires activities conducted indoors (outdoor storage requires special use).

## Financial / operational notes

**Grant funding:** Property owner awarded grant from Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity for water/sewer line installation; construction already begun. Grant requires minimum 50 acres zoned industrial.

**Developer requirements (per conditions):**
- Performance Bond or Letter-of-Credit required for public improvements prior to final plat recording
- Engineer's cost estimate required with construction plans
- All standard permit, inspection, tap-on, connection, recapture, basin fees due before final plat approval
- Sidewalks required along all public rights-of-way

**Infrastructure obligations:**
- Developer must construct/dedicate temporary roadway easement connecting Gateway Center Drive to Chrysler Drive
- Sanitary, drainage, and access easement dedication required within 30 days of final plat approval
- Subsurface drainage study required per subdivision code

**Tax/economic benefits:** Applicant cited sales tax revenue and employment opportunities as development benefits.
