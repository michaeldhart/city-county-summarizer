## The lede

- The board approved Kimberly Sardegna to fill the District 2 vacancy after debate over process, with Chairman Johnson defending the appointment and citing difficulty finding willing candidates; the motion passed with one abstention.
- The board approved the FY 2027 budget appropriation and property tax levy (Ordinance 26-28), with Finance Committee Chair Tom Walberg thanking staff and department heads for their work on the budget.
- Residents voiced strong opposition during public comment to the proposed Project Hawkeye data center near Genoa Road, citing concerns about farmland loss, water contamination, health impacts from diesel generators, and lack of county oversight.
- The board approved Change Order #34 for the courthouse project in the amount of $13,826.36 (reduced from the original amount after removing carpet and paint work), including security film for windows and other modifications.

## Attendance & housekeeping

All board members were present: Carl Johnson (Chairman), Josh Schumacher, Dave Corgrey (Cordray), Brian Schneider, Dave Wiltse, Lisa Patterson, Tom Walberg, Rodney Riley, Dana Logsdon, and Marion Thornberry.

Chairman Johnson recognized outgoing board members Dave Cordray, Dana Logsdon, Lisa Patterson, and former member Ryan Curry for their service to the county.

The board approved the September 17, 2026 and October 15, 2026 meeting minutes and the meeting agenda as presented.

## Decisions & votes

**Kimberly Sardegna appointment to District 2 vacancy (5.01):** Approved with one abstention. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Schumacher. Riley abstained. Thornberry moved to remove the item from the agenda, citing due process concerns and stating another candidate had expressed interest; Schneider seconded. That motion failed. Discussion included Riley arguing for more time to vet candidates and to allow the new board to make the appointment, saying two days' notice was insufficient. Wiltse, as vice chairman of the Republican Party, stated no bylaw required Central Committee approval. Johnson defended Sardegna's qualifications and said he had not been contacted by any other interested candidates, adding that Senator Severson wanted the seat filled quickly.

**Brian Amling to Zoning Board of Appeals Alternate #1:** Approved unanimously. Motion by Thornberry, seconded by Riley.

**Robert Goeddeke reappointment to Capron Rescue Squad:** Approved unanimously. Motion by Riley, seconded by Thornberry.

**General consent items (fund transfers, road projects, donations, raffles):** Approved unanimously. Motion by Riley, seconded by Thornberry.

**Electronic recycling contract with Dynamic Lifecycle Innovations:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Riley, seconded by Thornberry. Wiltse moved to amend the contract to restore an indemnification clause and allow the state's attorney to review before final execution; Schumacher seconded. Amendment passed unanimously. All voted yes on the amended contract.

**Preliminary Plat PP-13-2026 for Route 20 Storage:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Schneider, seconded by Cordray. Staff reported this was part of the previously approved planned development for storage units, with 14 conditions recommended by the Regional Planning Commission. All voted yes.

**Final Plat FP-14-2026 for Route 20 Storage:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Schumacher, seconded by Cordray. All voted yes.

**Change Order #34 for courthouse project:** Approved by roll call vote for $13,826.36. Motion as amended. Original motion included three items: PCO 135 ($4,817.89 for state's attorney lounge modifications, with most cost to be charged back to the mechanical consultant); PCO 136 ($13,347.47 for additional security film at probation, circuit clerk, and jury entrance); and PCO 138 (credit of $4,338.98 for removing masonry parging that could not be color-matched). Item 138 for jury assembly room carpet and paint was removed to get separate contractor quotes. Cordray and Riley questioned the high cost of security film; staff explained it provides ballistic resistance beyond standard tempered glass. All voted yes.

**Telcom audit with BHG Consulting, Inc.:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Riley, seconded by Thornberry. All voted yes.

**2027 Holiday Schedule:** Approved unanimously by voice vote. Motion by Riley, seconded by Schneider. Wiltse asked about the rationale for two Christmas holidays; Toben confirmed it was required by personnel policy and union contract.

**Claims in the amount of $1,295,501.51:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Schneider. Carl Johnson abstained from claims submitted by William Charles, Northern Illinois Service Co., Core & Main, Boone County Highway Department, and county departments regarding sewage, storm water, construction and excavation. All others voted yes.

**Purchase of 2027 Ford F-550 for $109,978 from Bob Ridings, Inc.:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Schneider. All voted yes.

**Resolution 26-34 supporting Squaw Prairie, Marengo and Lawrenceville Road Bridge Construction grant application:** Approved unanimously by voice vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Wiltse.

**IDOT Forcemain Temporary Use Permit:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Schumacher. All voted yes.

**Ordinance 26-28 FY 2027 Annual Budget Appropriation and Property Tax Levy:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Thornberry. Walberg thanked Toben and department heads for their work on the budget. All voted yes.

**CIRMA insurance renewal for $1,402,251:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Wiltse. All voted yes.

**Resolution 26-33 establishing Public Defender salary:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded by Schumacher. New Public Defender Aaron Bushemi introduced himself; he previously served the county from 2006 to 2017. All voted yes.

**Lawn maintenance and snow removal bid from Lawn Maintenance Services:** Approved by roll call vote. Motion by Walberg, seconded (unclear). Johnson noted prices from three years ago had not increased significantly. Logsdon thanked Lewis for assembling the bid. All voted yes.

**Employment contract for County Engineer Justin Krohn (following executive session):** Vote not recorded in transcript; executive session motion passed by roll call vote.

## Discussion items (no vote)

Staff presented Route 20 Storage plats as part of previously approved planned development with 14 conditions; most conditions had been met in the final plat revision. Spencer Hammer, the applicant, briefly addressed the board.

Courthouse architect Javier Saavedra and project manager Randy Darnell discussed Change Order #34 details via remote connection, explaining the state's attorney lounge sink issue, security film specifications, and the decision to remove parging work after three unsuccessful color mockups.

## Public comment

**Lisa Wolf** (401 East 6th Street, Belvidere) addressed board member Dana Logsdon regarding a Facebook comment he made on her October 16 post about voter registration. She read Logsdon's comment: "You know that if you register illegal immigrants and they vote that you can be held responsible now. Hate to see that happy little face in prison." Wolf said she took offense and felt it was a threat and possibly discriminatory, questioning whether he would have made the comment if she were white. She said she chose to address him in person rather than bring "an army of people" or pursue other avenues of complaint.

**Lisa Fitzgerald** (2913 Carlson Road, Belvidere) opposed Project Hawkeye, citing Resolution 2026-8 from March 2026 regarding jurisdictional transfer of Genoa Road between the county and city, which she found suspicious in timing. She noted Genoa Road was always a county road and the development area overlaps with the proposed data center location. She raised concerns about water contamination risk due to natural springs in Spring Township, noting data centers typically become outdated in three to five years, and questioned decommissioning plans as AI industry values decline.

**Rebecca Meyer** (3097 Garden Prairie Road) argued that Project Hawkeye's impacts extend beyond Belvidere city limits to county and township residents who have no vote on city leadership. She specifically called on District 1 board members (Schneider, Cordray, Schumacher, Wiltse) to advocate for constituents affected by the project, noting Schneider's role on agricultural conservation bodies tasked with protecting farmland. She urged board members to speak publicly at Belvidere City Council meetings before irreversible decisions are made.

**Mark Beinlich** (5485 Fern Hill Road, Belvidere) raised concerns about two Enbridge pipelines (carrying 50 million and 8 million gallons per day) running north-south near the proposed data center site, with 500-foot easements on each side. He questioned how construction could proceed and cited his experience witnessing a 1977 pipeline break involving 500,000 gallons of diesel fuel. He detailed health risks from diesel generator fumes, including lung cancer, respiratory disease, heart disease, and kidney damage, and worried about impacts on downwind residents. He also raised concerns about stormwater runoff, flooding, and potential increases in health insurance costs for residents living near data centers.

**Ruth Haberstitch** (8227 Fruit Farm Road) criticized officials for not researching data center impacts, citing a "320 pages" document. She referenced Virginia data showing electricity costs increased 287% and water costs 120% over five years near data centers. She discussed nitrate contamination from heated water discharge affecting children's brain development and causing cancers. She urged the board to propose a ban on data centers in Boone County and questioned the value of tax breaks when billion-dollar infrastructure would be needed.

**Kay Wilson** (525 Appleton Road, Belvidere) described driving the Fern Hill Road and Hilbert Road loop and being moved to tears thinking about residents potentially losing their homes. Growing up on a farm herself, she emphasized the value of rural life and the many homes and wooded wildlife areas that would be affected. She urged the board to consider how they would feel with "this monstrosity" in their backyard and to protect the Kishwaukee River, wells, and water supply, calling for rejection of Project Hawkeye and all data centers in Boone County.

## Notable moments

Marion Thornberry moved to remove Kimberly Sardegna's appointment from the agenda, citing failure to follow proper process through the Republican Central Committee. This sparked extended debate about vetting procedures, party bylaws, and statutory requirements for filling vacancies.

Rodney Riley gave a lengthy statement saying he had "never said this before" in two years on the board, but felt compelled to object to the rushed appointment process. He acknowledged the difficulty finding candidates but argued the new board taking office in December should make the appointment, stating "We have a new board coming in. We'll have a new chairman very soon." He said giving board members only two days to decide was insufficient.

Dave Wiltse, identifying himself as vice chairman and parliamentarian of the Republican Party, contradicted Thornberry's claim that party bylaws required Central Committee approval, stating that statutory process governs county board appointments. State's Attorney confirmed the appointment must only be of the same political party and within 60 days of resignation.

Chairman Johnson defended his appointment process, saying he talked to several people who declined to serve, that he is "not psychic" and cannot know someone is interested unless they contact him, and that Senator Severson as Republican Central Committee chair wanted the seat filled quickly. He called it "ludicrous" to deny Sardegna because someone else didn't follow proper procedure to express interest. He said his first choice had been to reappoint Lisa Patterson for another two years but she was unable to serve.

Lisa Wolf's public comment directly confronting Dana Logsdon about his Facebook comment created visible tension. Wolf methodically read both her original post and Logsdon's response verbatim, stated she felt threatened and possibly discriminated against, but said she chose to come alone "not in vulnerability, but in strength" with "God is with me."

Chairman Carl Johnson gave an extended farewell speech after 18 years on the board (20 years total involvement), thanking staff, previous board chairs (Susan Anderson, Kathy Ward, Bob Walberg), and especially former county administrator Ken Terrinoni, whom he called "a tremendous force" who "threw around nickels like they were manhole covers" but always protected taxpayers. Johnson reflected on navigating the recession and cutting $1 million from the budget, emphasized that board members can disagree on issues but should respect each other outside the chamber, and thanked his wife and family for their support.

Josh Schumacher praised Chairman Johnson before executive session, noting Johnson had given awards to everyone else but not mentioned his own service, calling his work "fantastic."

Riley and Wiltse announced they had provided all board members with educational materials about data centers, with Wiltse describing an extensive industry meeting with hard questions asked. Johnson emphasized the importance of educating the board before making any decisions on data centers, stating "we have to educate ourselves so that we can then pass that on to the public." Walberg said he sent Johnson links to three University of Illinois videos on data centers to distribute.

Tom Walberg thanked Becky Toben and all department heads for work on the FY 2027 budget, calling it "a really nice budget."

Lisa Patterson gave a bittersweet report on her last Community Building board meeting, describing the new 11-by-20-foot screen with 20,000-lumen projector, completed roof repairs, and upcoming door replacements, saying "I am just going to miss these people" while holding up a mug that says "Boone County place to call home." She added "that remains to be seen" about whether she'd have to move.

New Public Defender Aaron Bushemi briefly introduced himself, noting he previously served Boone County from 2006 to 2017 and was "very, very happy to be back."

Chairman Johnson announced his farewell party would be December 7th from 4:00 to 6:15 p.m. in the community room, immediately before the 6:30 organizational meeting. Someone noted December 7th as "a day that will live in infamy."

The board entered executive session for personnel matters and was expected to vote on County Engineer Justin Krohn's employment contract upon return, though that vote was not recorded in the provided transcript.
