## TL;DR
- Council approved annexation and zoning changes for Rush Power Systems property at Hawkeye Drive and Irene Road after public hearing with no public comment
- Approved stop sign changes at two intersections after public testimony: four-way stop at 5th Avenue/West 6th Street, and amended Caswell Street/East 5th Street to two-way stop on Caswell (instead of on 5th Street as originally proposed)
- Approved multiple police and fire equipment purchases, including body armor plates, handguns, and breathing air system for Fire Station 2
- Authorized $2.1M engineering contract for Appleton Road Grade Separation Project, funded almost entirely by grant ($2.08M grant, $17K city funds)

## Discussion items

**Stop Sign Debate:** Extended discussion on Caswell/5th Street intersection. Public commenters Lynda Lenhardt and John Fish advocated for stop signs due to speeding and accidents. Council debated whether stops should be on 5th or Caswell; Peterson noted the long block on Caswell warrants traffic control. Mayor noted stop signs don't guarantee compliance (like speed limits) and committed to deploying speed trailer and increased enforcement. Director Anderson clarified stop signs aren't intended as speed control devices.

**Video Gaming License:** Discussion about bar/gaming at gas station (717 North State). Hoiness expressed concern about alcohol availability at gas stations and impaired driving. Staff noted several gas stations already have gaming; special use limited to specific small area to prevent full bar conversion. 35 active gaming licenses (of 50 allowed); 7 closed in past year, trending down (possibly due to Hard Rock Casino opening).

**Grant Funding Success:** Peterson praised administration and Public Works for securing $2.08M in grant funding for Appleton Road project (98.9% grant-funded), noting difficulty obtaining such funds "after Doge has done its work."

**Contract Improvements:** City Attorney Drella reported negotiating two changes to Lexipol contract: payment terms aligned with Illinois Prompt Payment Act (not arbitrary 30 days) and removed automatic renewal provision to ensure one-year term with optional renewal.

## Public comment

**Lynda Lenhardt** (resident, corner of Caswell and 5th Street, 31 years): Requested stop signs due to multiple accidents in yard, speeding (35-40 mph), young children in area. Recent accident nearly hit husband mowing; expressed safety concerns. Noted yield signs ignored, requested two-way or four-way stop and increased police patrols and speed limit signs.

**John Fish** (900 block Caswell, 39 years): Supported stop signs on Caswell Street. Noted long block (904-1224 Caswell, effectively 2-3 block length). Previously t-boned at 4th/Caswell when it was yield (now four-way stop). Noted high school students using Caswell/Warren to avoid school traffic, increasing volume. Recommended four-way stop but accepted two-way on Caswell. Pointed out if stops placed on 5th, would create six consecutive stop signs from East Avenue to South State. Emphasized safety: "only been two accidents there in the last two years, but there's been six people injured."

**Mayor Morris** reminded attendees about National Day of Prayer events Thursday, May 7, 2026 (8am prayer breakfast at community building, noon at Big Thunder Park).
