## The lede

- The Committee of the Whole approved designating Perry Elementary School at 633 W. Perry Street as a local landmark, recognizing the 1897 building as one of the oldest continuously operating schools in Illinois.
- The council approved a $31,000 contract with C.E.S. Inc. for engineering work on a federally funded sidewalk safety project around Washington Academy.
- Mayor Clinton Morris addressed residents' concerns about a potential data center project, stating he has not approved any project and lacks details on water, power, and noise impacts.
- The city accepted a clean audit for fiscal year ending April 30, 2026, with no material misstatements or compliance issues.

## Decisions & votes

**Perry Elementary School Landmark Designation**  
Motion to approve the designation of the school at 633 W. Perry Street as a local landmark.  
Vote: 9-0 (Albertini, Brereton, Fleury, Frank, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, Peterson, Stevens all voted yes)

**Safe Routes to School Engineering Contract**  
Motion to approve a proposal from C.E.S. Inc. for $31,000 for preliminary and design engineering for the 2025 Safe Routes to Belvidere Schools project around Washington Academy, to be paid from Public Benefit Funds.  
Vote: 9-0 (Brereton, Fleury, Frank, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, Peterson, Stevens, Albertini all voted yes)

**City Audit Acceptance**  
Motion to accept and approve the audit as presented by Sikich for the fiscal year ending April 30, 2026.  
Voice vote: Approved unanimously

**City Hall Flooring Replacement**  
Motion to approve a proposal from Lonnie's Carpet Max for $63,845 for interior flooring at City Hall, to be paid from Impact Fees.  
Vote: 9-0 (Fleury, Frank, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, Peterson, Stevens, Albertini, Brereton all voted yes)

**Fire Department Open House Street Closure**  
Motion to authorize closure of State Street from Meadow Street to the north side of the State Street Bridge on Saturday, October 10, 2026, from 10:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., and to authorize use of the State Street Bridge as a medical helicopter landing location.  
Voice vote: Approved

## Discussion items

**Data Center Concerns**  
Three residents spoke against a potential data center project, expressing concerns about water usage, electricity consumption, noise impacts on farm animals, and the speed of the project's advancement. Mayor Morris responded that no project has been approved, the city lacks details on water, power, and noise, and any proposal would go through the planning and zoning commission and city council. He stated a letter he wrote in May 2025 was not a commitment but part of his regular outreach to court development. Morris noted that Cherry Valley may be considering a data center project in Boone County.

**Perry School History and Significance**  
Community Development Planner Gina DelRose explained that Perry School was built in 1897 to serve families moving to Belvidere for jobs at the National Sewing Machine Company. The school is named after Commodore Matthew Calbraith Perry, who opened trade with Japan in 1853. Unlike sister schools Lincoln (demolished) and Logan (heavily altered), Perry retains much of its original architectural character. Mayor Morris noted the city provided the school district $520,817 in impact fees, which was not used to maintain Perry School before it was closed.

**Audit Results**  
Nick Bava from Sikich CPA reported the city received an unmodified "clean" opinion on its financial statements. The city spent $4.3 million in grants, including $3.2 million in federal grants (triggering additional compliance testing), with no instances of non-compliance or questioned costs. Major federal expenditures included $1 million from the American Rescue Plan Act and $2 million through the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency for drinking water improvements.

**Perry School Impact on Neighborhood**  
Alderman Peterson stated that Perry School will be a catalyst for the neighborhood, for better or worse, and abandoning it would hurt property values in an area already affected by the loss of the neighborhood school and Park Pool.

## Financial / operational notes

The 2025 Safe Routes to School project has an estimated construction cost of $247,011 with a grant award of $249,346. The engineering work ($31,000) will be paid from Public Benefit Funds. Director Keck noted the project timing aligns with Fifth Avenue reconstruction, and the grant essentially provides free money for additional sidewalk work that would have been required anyway.

The city hall flooring project received four bids ranging from $63,845 to $96,614. The carpet is original to the 20-year-old building and shows significant wear and water staining from previous roof leaks. The project will include porcelain tile in high-traffic common areas and carpet in office wings, paid from Impact Fees.

Building Department issued 126 permits in the previous month totaling $41,080 in fees (16 commercial permits for $16,500 and 110 residential permits for $25,200). Code enforcement conducted four forced mowings with 15 cases still in progress.

The audit showed the city collected Impact Fees that were provided to the school district ($520,817), which the mayor stated were not used for Perry School maintenance before its closure.

## Public comment

Teresa Stein said she has lived in Belvidere for 20 years and opposes the data center project due to concerns about water usage, electricity consumption, noise impacts on farm animals, and potential harm to the town's character.

Bonita Gerdes said she has lived at 4777 Stone Corey Road since 1998 and is frightened by how fast the data center discussions are moving. She cited 200 pages of municipal records and discussions dating to late 2024, questioned whether Belvidere would be a "guinea pig" for a large-scale project, and expressed concerns about noise traveling across farmland and impacts on her horses, barn cats, dog, and wildlife.

Ruth Haberstich challenged the mayor's jurisdiction for annexing portions of Genoa Road, citing Illinois statute 65 ILCS 5/7-1-1 requiring contiguous territories and stating the tollway bridge separates city from county. She questioned why residents were not informed earlier about the project and asked officials to save farmlands, water systems, and the area's way of life.

## Notable moments

Mayor Morris delivered an extended response to data center concerns, stating he has lived in Belvidere for 57 years and has not received any money from developers or charged taxpayers for gas or meals when meeting with potential investors. He emphasized the need to court development to avoid raising property taxes, noting the city has kept its tax levy at zero for four of the past five years. Morris cited previous controversial projects like Walmart that faced opposition but ultimately benefited the community.

Alderman Brereton repeatedly asked staff members "What can you tell us about Project Hawkeye?" receiving responses that no concrete details are available. The mayor thanked him sarcastically for his trust after the third such question.

The mayor addressed Facebook rumors that he received a house in Florida and payments from developers, denying all such claims.

Alderman Peterson raised safety concerns about landing a medical helicopter on the State Street Bridge for the fire department open house, noting the risk despite the visual appeal. Fire Captain Swanson said the department would work with the chief to address the concerns before the council meeting.

One member of the public attempted to speak during the mayor's response but was told it was "out of order" since he had not registered during the public comment period.
