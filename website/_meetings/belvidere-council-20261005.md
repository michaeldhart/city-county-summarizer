## The lede

- City Council passed a special use permit for a bank drive-through at 1862 Crystal Parkway, designated Perry Elementary School as a local historic landmark, and approved amendments to the city's subdivision ordinance to streamline lot consolidations.
- The council approved two intergovernmental agreements allocating maintenance costs for the Irene Road Bridge, with the city and county each paying 50% of a $179,637 reimbursement to the Illinois Tollway and sharing future capital costs.
- Public comment was dominated by residents opposing a proposed data center project known as "Project Hawkeye," with speakers citing concerns about water use, noise pollution, child safety risks from AI, agricultural land loss, and a lack of transparency around a $150,000 fund.
- The council also approved nearly $2 million in infrastructure projects, including wastewater treatment plant repairs and a stormwater management project.

## Decisions & votes

**Ordinance #784H** (Special use permit for bank drive-through at 1862 Crystal Parkway): Passed 10-0.

**Ordinance #785H** (Amendments to subdivision ordinance): Passed with 8 voting yes and 2 abstentions (Brereton and Albertini). The amendments clarify the Illinois Plat Act exemptions, add a definition for "consolidation plats," adjust inspection fees from 2% to 3%, and create a streamlined process for combining adjacent lots without creating new streets or easements.

**Ordinance #786H** (Historic landmark designation for Perry School at 633 W. Perry Street): Passed 10-0.

**Resolution #2026-21** (Intergovernmental agreement between Belvidere, Boone County, and Flora Township for Irene Road Bridge maintenance): Passed 10-0. The city and county each pay 50% of $179,637 in current project costs; Flora Township handles routine maintenance while the city and county split future capital costs 50/50.

**Resolution #2026-22** (Four-way intergovernmental agreement adding Illinois Tollway to Irene Road Bridge agreement): Passed 10-0.

**Motion to waive bidding for wastewater treatment plant disc filter repairs**: Passed 10-0.

**Motion to approve $159,011.41 contract with Veolia Water Technologies** for disc filter vessel rebuild, paid from Sewer Depreciation Account: Passed 10-0.

**Motion to approve $17,540 design engineering change order with ARC Design Resources** for Kishwaukee Riverfront Path Extension, paid from grant and capital funds: Passed with 9 yes votes and 1 abstention (Brereton).

**Motion to approve $1,987,322.50 contract with N-Trak Group** for Allen Street Pond Outlet to River Project, paid from capital funds: Passed 10-0.

## Discussion items

Superintendent Dr. Cassandra Schug of Belvidere CUSD #100 requested the city consider installing stop signs at East 6th Street and Pearson Parkway near Mein Elementary School, similar to what was done at West 6th Street and Fifth Avenue near Washington Academy. She also mentioned the district's opposition to Perry School's landmark designation would be addressed through partnership discussions with the city.

Mayor Morris announced the newly reconstituted City-County Coordinating Committee will meet quarterly starting October 20, 2026, at the Boone County Administration building. City representatives are Alderwoman Frank and Aldermen Peterson and Montalbano.

Community Development Planner Gina DelRose explained the subdivision ordinance amendments were prompted by ongoing issues with property owners combining parcels through county PIN consolidations rather than formal plats, creating situations where buildings became non-conforming when individual lots were later sold off. The new "plat of consolidation" process was specifically needed for properties like the Hamblock Ford site that includes four parcels.

## Financial / operational notes

The total local entity share owed to the Illinois Tollway for the Irene Road Bridge project is $179,637.37, split 50/50 between the city and county, with payments due in two installments: 50% within 30 days of an invoice issued no earlier than May 1, 2027, and the remainder within 30 days of a final invoice issued no earlier than May 1, 2028.

Flora Township Road District will handle routine maintenance of the Irene Road Bridge including snow removal, pothole repair, guardrail repair, and drainage facilities. The city and county will split non-routine capital expenditures 50/50. These local maintenance obligations will transfer entirely to the city when the bridge approaches become part of the city road system.

The wastewater treatment plant disc filters are 15 years old, exceeding their 10-year expected lifespan. Filters #2 and #3 require immediate replacement at $159,011.41, while filters #1 and #4 repairs can be deferred and budgeted over the next two years.

Inspection fees for developments will be 3% of the approved engineer's estimate of construction costs, with any excess inspection costs paid before final acceptance of public improvements.

The September 21, 2026 expenditures were higher than normal due to fire and police pension payments and the opening of a $2 million certificate of deposit.

## Public comment

Ten residents spoke in opposition to a proposed data center project known as "Project Hawkeye." Mike Dwarzinski cited global data center counts showing the U.S. has 5,427 data centers compared to Germany's 529. Lisa Fitzgerald described contamination at a Beaver Dam data center that turned creek water white with chemicals, making it unsafe for livestock.

Cassandra Roadie stated AI is being exploited to generate child sexual abuse material, with over 7,000 cases reported in the last two years, and argued that approving the data center would make the council complicit in enabling child exploitation through AI.

Michael Novak formally opposed a $150,000 fund created by Datawatt Energy that can be used for "miscellaneous expenditures" and continually replenished, requesting transparency on how those funds are spent.

Billy Sparacino, a teacher at Cherry Valley, said the district has had six suicides in two weeks, many linked to online content, and noted that while AI tools like ChatGPT have legitimate uses, the community shouldn't enable problematic applications by hosting a data center.

Claudia Roadie discussed the health impacts of continuous low-frequency noise from hyperscale data center chillers, cooling towers, and generators, citing Harvard Health research showing such noise increases arterial inflammation and cardiovascular problems. She noted four schools are within a mile and a half of the proposed site and questioned whether economic benefits justify health impacts, particularly on children.

Ann O'Haire appealed to the council to work collaboratively with rural residents, noting that 4-H members from the county contribute to city activities and vice versa, and that both communities benefit from partnership.

Katie Boughton said Freedom of Information Act documents show planning for the data center has been ongoing for some time but aldermen were not copied on most correspondence. She quoted Bill Gates from a New York Times interview one week prior stating AI is "the most dangerous thing that humans have ever gone near" due to cyber and bioterrorism risks.

Ruth Haberstich provided extensive research on Datawatt Energy, describing it as a power infrastructure developer with 15 gigawatts of global energy projects. She said the proposed Project Hawkeye would be a 3-gigawatt hyperscale campus with a 500-megawatt hydrogen fuel cell, and that Datawatt partners with solar farm installations across Illinois specifically to power its data centers, with the electricity not going to the public.

## Notable moments

Mayor Morris recognized John Mundy as the 2026 Heritage Days Parade Honorary Marshal, presenting him with a plaque thanking him for his years of service to Belvidere Youth Baseball and the community.

Judge C. Robert Tobin III and Boone County Probation Director Amy Gallano accepted the Family Violence Awareness Month proclamation. Judge Tobin emphasized that domestic violence is a community issue requiring coordinated response from residents, police, prosecutors, courts, and treatment providers. Gallano announced Belvidere Police Officer Michelle Bogdanis would be recognized at a "Shining the Light" ceremony on October 6 for her work with domestic violence victims and survivors.

The council held a 10-minute executive session to discuss collective bargaining matters related to union contracts with fraternal police, firefighters, and/or public works employees. The city attorney clarified the purpose for the public record before entering the closed session.
