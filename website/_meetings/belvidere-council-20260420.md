## The lede
- **Ordinance 754H passed (8–1)** establishing inspection of non-residential and multi-family structures, limited to common areas (not individual tenant units); Northwest Illinois Alliance of Realtors raised concerns about clarity on Fourth Amendment rights and enforcement procedures
- **Approved $310,500 engineering contract** for 5th Avenue storm sewer overflow reconstruction project to address chronic flooding
- **Passed four resolutions** including America250 commemoration support, electrical/plumbing inspection contractor agreements, and State Line Narcotics Team interagency agreement
- **Approved routine expenditures** totaling ~$3.3M (General/Special Funds: $2.5M; Water/Sewer: $776K)

## Discussion items

**Building inspection ordinance (754H) – extensive debate:**
- Neely Erickson (Northwest Illinois Alliance of Realtors) opposed, citing gaps: ordinance doesn't explicitly state Fourth Amendment right to refuse entry; lacks clarity on notices, timelines, appeals, enforcement; unclear property identification process, occupancy rules, and "not obligated to inspect" language
- Fire Chief Schadle clarified: does not change current practice dating to at least 1985; allows administrative search warrant if property owner refuses routine annual inspection (per *Camara* case precedent); inspections cover common areas only—hallways, shared utility/mechanical spaces—not individual tenant units
- City Attorney Drella explained legal framework: *Camara* court allows easier administrative warrants when municipality has established inspection routine; refusal of inspection alone constitutes probable cause for warrant; Fourth Amendment protects individual residences, not commercial/industrial spaces
- Alderman Stevens noted 25-year fire department history conducting common-area inspections
- Alderman Brereton attempted to refer back to committee (failed for lack of second)
- Discussion addressed scope: commercial/industrial properties inspected in entirety; multi-family limited to common areas to protect residential privacy

**5th Avenue storm sewer project:**
- Alderman Peterson noted April rainfall 3" above average, making project timely
- Mayor Morris praised project as legacy addressing decades of flooding
- Alderman Stevens observed significant water already in new retention pond; Director Anderson explained phased approach with future lower-level release pipe to river

## Public comment

**Neeley Erickson, Government Affairs Director, Northwest Illinois Alliance of Realtors** (representing 1,200 members):
- Opposed Ord. 754H as written
- Acknowledged Fire Chief's helpful explanation and appreciated floor amendment clarifying multi-family scope
- **Gaps identified:**
  - Ordinance doesn't explicitly state property owners/tenants can refuse inspection under Fourth Amendment
  - Administrative search warrant process mentioned in packet but not in ordinance text
  - No clarity on penalties (or lack thereof) for exercising refusal right
  - Procedures (timeline, notices, appeals) buried in "Bible-thick" Chapter 22 fire code, not ordinance
  - Unclear property identification/registration system, outreach plan, effective date
  - Ambiguous occupancy rules (can unit be occupied before inspection?)
  - "Not obligated to inspect" language creates inconsistency/potential liability concerns
- **Requested:** Hold ordinance to address gaps; add simple language stating warrant process and no penalties for refusal
- Emphasized support for safety goals but need for clarity to avoid interpretation problems
