## The lede

- The Committee of the Whole voted 8-2 to direct the city attorney to draft a resolution ordering the mayor and administration to cease all negotiations with Data Watt Energy on a proposed data center known as Project Hawkeye.
- Mayor Clinton Morris stated he has not received detailed project specifications or a formal proposal, and emphasized he has not signed a non-disclosure agreement regarding the project.
- Fourteen residents spoke during public comment opposing the data center, citing concerns about water consumption, noise pollution, electricity costs, and impacts on agricultural land.
- The council approved renewing the city's property, casualty, and workers' compensation insurance with IML RMA for $805,170.17, a 1% increase from the prior year.

## Decisions & votes

**Property, Casualty & Workers' Compensation Insurance Renewal**
Motion by Alderman Peterson, second by Alderman Stevens, to approve renewal with IML RMA for January 1, 2027 through January 1, 2028, for $805,170.17 (including $2,000 in IML dues), paid in full by November 13, 2026 to receive a 1% discount. Voice vote carried unanimously.

**Motion to Table Project Hawkeye Discussion**
Motion by Alderman Peterson, second by Alderman Frank, to table discussion until an application has been received and a public forum can be held. Roll call vote: 6-4 against. Ayes: Frank, Hoiness, Peterson, Stevens. Nays: Albertini, Brereton, Fleury, Gramkowski, McGee, Montalbano. Motion to table failed.

**Resolution Directing Cessation of Data Center Negotiations**
Motion by Alderman Brereton, second by Alderman Gramkowski, to direct the city attorney to draft a resolution explicitly directing the mayor and city administration to cease all negotiations, discussions, or meetings with Data Watt Energy to build a data center (Project Hawkeye). Roll call vote: 8-2 in favor. Ayes: Brereton, Fleury, Gramkowski, Hoiness, McGee, Montalbano, Stevens, Albertini. Nays: Frank, Peterson. Motion carried.

## Discussion items

**Project Hawkeye Data Center Proposal**
Mayor Morris delivered a lengthy statement asserting no final decision or agreement has been reached and no concrete plan has been submitted. He said he is awaiting detailed project specifications in writing. Morris explained that a $150,000 advance from Data Watt was intended to cover the city's costs for professional vetting services (environmental, engineering, legal) so taxpayers would not pay for due diligence. He stated he has not offered tax incentives and that the state rescinded data center tax benefits in June 2026. Morris said the project would not be in the enterprise zone.

City Attorney Mike Drella raised concerns about whether the council has authority to prohibit the mayor from communicating with third parties, noting that in Illinois the mayor serves as CEO and generally the council cannot restrict the mayor's discussions, though the council must approve any binding agreements. Drella said he would research the legal question before the next meeting.

Several aldermen stated their opposition to the data center based on constituent feedback. Alderman Albertini, Alderman Fleury, and Alderman McGee said residents in their wards oppose the project. Alderman Gramkowski cited examples of other Illinois cities (Rochelle, Naperville, South Elgin, Barrington Hills) that rejected or saw withdrawal of data center proposals.

Alderman Peterson advocated for conducting a public forum similar to what Cherry Valley did with a Microsoft data center proposal, allowing detailed presentation and question-and-answer with all stakeholders before making decisions.

Morris defended his economic development approach, stating the city has had more growth in the past five years than the previous 60 combined, and that the city has held tax levy increases to an average of 1% annually despite 22% cumulative inflation since 2021. He said large developments help the city avoid raising taxes on residents.

**Public Comment Themes**
Residents expressed opposition based on: increased electricity costs (citing a Bloomberg study showing up to 267% spikes in data center hubs), water consumption (1-5 million gallons daily), infrastructure costs passed to ratepayers, noise pollution (85-96 decibels), health impacts from air pollution and diesel generators, strain on emergency services, threat to agricultural land and wells, property value impacts, and lack of representation for county residents outside city limits who cannot vote on city officials making the decision.

One speaker questioned who initiated talks between the city and Data Watt, whether research was done on negative effects before accepting the $150,000, and how fire departments would handle a data center fire. Another noted two new businesses (Generac Power Systems and NextGen Manufacturing) specializing in data center services are opening in Belvidere in 2027, suggesting the timing is not coincidental.

A resident referenced Mayor Morris's campaign statements about honest leadership and representing constituents' interests, contrasting them with his communications with Dairyland officials in December 2024 and a May 22, 2025 letter of support to David Halligan while running for re-election without mentioning the data center to voters.

## Financial / operational notes

The Ida Public Library requested a tax levy of $895,340, an increase of $42,635 or 5% over the prior year. This was presented for information only; the council must approve the tax levy by the last Tuesday in December.

Budget and Finance Officer Sarah Turnipseed reported she will present the tax levy announcement on November 2, with discussion on November 9, first reading November 16, and second reading December 7. The levy must be passed and filed by December 29, 2026.

A general fund certificate of deposit with over $1 million is up for renewal in November. Turnipseed is checking rates to get the best return.

The Illinois Municipal League released state shared revenue estimates showing mostly flat projections overall. Income tax distributions were revised up slightly, replacement tax is projected to improve, but use tax continues well below prior years due to state law changes shifting revenues to other taxes rather than true loss. Turnipseed will incorporate conservative estimates into the fiscal year 2028 budget.

The property insurance renewal with IML RMA represented only a 1% increase. The city has been a member of IML RMA since 1981. The request for proposals was 22 pages covering 80+ locations, 124 vehicles, equipment inventory, payroll by job class, and eight years of claims history. Only one competing proposal was received from Robertson Ryan Insurance, which came in $81,325 higher than IML RMA with the early payment discount.

## Public comment

Fourteen residents spoke during the public comment period (the mayor limited total public comment to 45 minutes rather than allowing five minutes each for 25 people who signed up):

**Ruth Haberstich** cited Census data on poverty and elderly populations in Belvidere and Boone County, then detailed water infrastructure costs (estimated $10-58 billion nationwide to support data centers) and electricity cost spikes (up to 267% over five years in Virginia, Georgia, and Arizona data center hubs). She said cities must issue rate hikes on residential customers to pay for water system upgrades.

**Norma Estep** noted that Generac Power Systems and NextGen Manufacturing, both specializing in data center services, are opening in Belvidere in 2027, suggesting the timing coinciding with the data center proposal is not coincidental. She said residents don't want noise, water depletion, pollution, higher electric bills, or reduced property values.

**Cheryl Bock** asked who initiated talks in 2024, whether the city researched negative effects before accepting $150,000, whether Belvidere residents would benefit from incentives if the center is built in Cherry Valley, who will pay to redrill wells, whether the fire department has adequate equipment and personnel, how residents would be notified of chemical leaks, and whether the structure could withstand an EF4 or EF5 tornado.

**Rebecca Meyer** said she and neighbors outside city limits have no representation because they cannot vote for city officials making the decision, yet share the same air, water, and roads. She cited constant noise pollution, water quality threats, and strain on the power grid. She quoted from Mayor Morris's campaign Facebook page about honest leadership and representing residents' best interests, contrasting this with his December 2024 communications with Dairyland and May 22, 2025 letter of support written while running for re-election without mentioning the data center.

**Teresa Stein** detailed pollution concerns including carbon and air emissions from fossil fuel energy, diesel backup generators, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, fine particulate matter, water consumption of 1-5 million gallons daily, chemical discharges including PFAS ("forever chemicals"), noise of 85-96 decibels, and challenges for emergency responders dealing with fires at data centers with high voltage systems and lithium ion battery arrays.

**Crystal Alexander** said data centers are among the most energy-intensive facilities on Earth, with consumption projected to double by 2030, driving rising utility bills. She noted concerns about grid reliability, brownouts, power shortages, reliance on diesel generators, water consumption in drought-stressed regions, noise pollution (constant buzzing), loss of agricultural land (which takes 500 years to create one inch of topsoil), few permanent jobs (only 20 required for Illinois tax credits lasting 20 years), and electronic waste from brief hardware replacement cycles.

**Marie Novak** said the mayor lied when he said the data center is not on the agenda when it literally is. She said documents released by the city start mid-conversation and the first email from December 2024 says "Are you available for a meeting?" rather than an introduction. She urged the city to release the full extent of communications with Data Watt. She said the city promised to release more documents but it has been two months with nothing new despite Data Watt giving the city at least $150,000.

**Mike Novak** said planning work is visible in fields around his home and FOIA requests revealed a massive development that will be not more than 30 feet from his bedroom window. He asked how this is possible when ordinances protect the land as agricultural. He said no amount of tax rebates, school funding, or gift cards can replace the community. He said construction jobs will be temporary and the data center will likely rescind promises. He said neighboring counties are issuing restrictions on data centers while Boone County is not listening to residents. He asked the city to rescind the reimbursement agreement and urged the county to stand up for residents.

**Renee Keeney** explained that PJM Interconnection operates the electrical grid for 13 states including northern Illinois. She said demand from large load consumers like data centers is outpacing grid capacity, affecting reliability and cost. She said the Citizens Utility Board reports cost per kilowatt hour has increased 53% over two years in anticipation of data center energy needs, with another 29-50% increase expected. She said consumers will absorb infrastructure costs, and any property tax relief would be offset by increased electric bills, making it a "net zero."

**Scott Zellmer** said 75% of Americans oppose data center development and more than 500 U.S. counties and municipalities have passed bans, including Rockford with a six-month moratorium. He recommended Belvidere declare at least a six-month moratorium and hold a referendum including input from those whose land would be annexed. He discussed the "impending AI bubble," comparing it to the 2008 housing bubble, saying if the bubble bursts and valuable farmland has been annexed, families displaced, and health problems arise, "it is us that will never recover."

**Greg Paciga** said he liquidated assets from a previous life and moved to Belvidere expecting it to be his final home, investing significantly in his property. He said the data center will be directly across the street, making his children's inheritance "worth squat." He addressed the council: "If you guys can't make the right decision for your constituents and for my neighbors here, I really don't want to look forward to the vote return on your next election."

**Mark Beinlich** said two pipelines (42-inch and 20-inch) carrying 180,000 and 50.4 million barrels per day run through the proposed data center property. He asked if the city has talked to the pipeline company and described witnessing a pipeline break in the 1970s that required fire department presence 24/7 for a month. He raised concerns about staging thousands of trucks for construction and stormwater management (8 million gallons from one inch of rain on 300 acres). He said he bought his house to die in it and doesn't want "all this BS."

**Luanne Kasper** focused on noise from cooling fans, HVAC systems, thousands of server fans, rooftop units, chillers, diesel generators, and vibrations creating constant "tonal drones" that run 365 days a year. She said low-frequency rumbles do not muffle easily and cause sleep disruption, stress, and anxiety. She said local noise ordinances are built for short-term disturbances, not continuous industrial hums.

**Paul McCaffrey** said he requested on October 15 that a question-and-answer session between the public and officials be added to the agenda. He said City Clerk Erica Bluege told him the mayor has sole discretion, and City Attorney Drella said officials are not allowed to talk and would give them legal advice not to reply. McCaffrey disputed this, saying other municipalities engage in public comment and the Open Meetings Act does not prohibit such interaction. He sent the request via email to the mayor and all aldermen but it was not added to the agenda. He received four replies; Alderman Frank said she could not speak to him individually because of the Open Meetings Act, which McCaffrey said is false. He raised concerns about substantial increases in electric bills in areas where data centers have been constructed.

## Notable moments

Mayor Morris invoked his mother, saying "I trust my mother, everybody else puts it in writing," when explaining he does not have written details about the data center proposal.

The mayor was repeatedly interrupted during his lengthy statement by audience members, with one person named Randy being asked to leave if order could not be maintained. Randy replied, "You had your five minutes and you gave us—" before being cut off. Another audience member called the meeting "a joke because no matter what you say, it's going to pass anyway."

When Mayor Morris said the council represents 25,339 city residents, an audience member shouted, "You are not even our mayor," referring to county residents outside city limits. Morris responded that the city pays county taxes but the county doesn't pay city taxes.

After the 8-2 vote directing cessation of negotiations, there was applause. Alderman Brereton noted the motion was drafted with assistance from "a former mayor of 16 years" who "sat in that chair for 16 years."

Alderman Albertini told Mayor Morris, "You put forth an ultimatum to them that either a data center or pay higher taxes. I think they'd rather pay the higher taxes," which drew applause.

Alderman Frank gave a closing statement saying people are angry with her for not stating how she will vote, explaining she takes detailed notes, serves on committees, and wanted to go through the process, but "this is who I am as a older person."

A resident named Scott Boller from Baldwin spoke after the motion to adjourn, saying he watched various aldermen during the meeting and "there was one person in this group up here that had no interest in this meeting at all, that was too busy with her phone and iPad." He also said "the more tax money the county receives, the more they spend" and suggested "if Illinois wants a Hawkeye data center, put it in Cook County," which drew applause.

The meeting lasted until 8:23 p.m.
