## The lede

- The Boone County Conservation District board approved a resolution honoring retiring attorney Deb Loos for 35 years of service to the district.
- The board heard a presentation from Renee Keeney of Keeney Creek Farm seeking permission to run underground electrical lines across the Long Prairie Trail to reach a buildable parcel.
- Executive Director Josh Sage presented a preliminary levy request showing a 3.3% increase, or $47,500, over the prior year, with final approval scheduled for November.
- The board entered executive session to discuss pending litigation and personnel matters but took no action afterward.

## Decisions & votes

**Executive session minutes maintained closed**: The board voted 5-0 to keep executive session minutes from March 18, 2026 and September 16, 2025 closed for an additional six months.

**September 15, 2026 meeting minutes approved**: The board voted 5-0 to approve the minutes as presented.

**Resolution No. 10-2026A approved**: The board voted 5-0 to approve a resolution recognizing Attorney Deb Loos' retirement and 35 years of service to the district.

**Accounts payable approved**: The board voted 5-0 to pay expenses due through October 20, 2026.

## Discussion items

**Long Prairie Trail electrical crossing**: Renee Keeney requested a license and easement agreement to allow ComEd to install underground electrical lines across the Long Prairie Trail using directional boring. The lines would connect a transformer near one parcel to a buildable lot on another parcel of Keeney Creek Farm. No action was taken.

**ComEd soil boring project**: ComEd contacted the district in spring 2026 about converting existing wooden transmission line posts to a single steel post system. The company requested access to district easements to perform soil borings. Executive Director Sage said he asked ComEd to work on frozen ground and avoid the growing season to protect wetlands and wildlife. No action was taken.

**Levy presentation**: Executive Director Sage presented draft levy estimates showing a 3.3% increase totaling $47,500 over the previous year. The final levy ordinance will be presented for approval at the November meeting.

**Executive session**: The board entered executive session at 4:25 p.m. and returned to open session at 4:45 p.m. to discuss pending litigation and personnel matters. Former legal counsel attended the executive session. No action was taken.

## Financial / operational notes

**September 2026 financial reports**: The board placed the September 2026 financial reports on file subject to audit.

**Facility updates**: Director of Land and Facilities Hunter Cejka reported that painting at the Pavilion has begun with a goal of completing restoration by spring 2027. All equipment is operational.

**Controlled burn training**: Director of Natural Resources Frank Rabbit said he has coordinated with Fire District 2 Chief Adams to have fire personnel present at a controlled burn for educational purposes.

**Congressional meeting**: Executive Director Sage and Mark Freedlund met with U.S. Representative Bill Foster at the Nature Center for an hour-long discussion about the district's mission and the Nature Center Expansion Project.

**Volunteer appreciation event**: The district will host a volunteer appreciation event on Friday, November 6, featuring the premiere of a centerpiece movie and public service announcements.
