## The lede

- The Planning & Zoning Commission unanimously approved a special use permit for a drive-through bank to be built at 1862 Crystal Parkway, near the I-90 interchange
- The commission also unanimously approved changes to the city's subdivision rules to better control how land can be split or combined
- Public comment at the meeting focused on concerns about a potential data center, though no such case was on the agenda
- Both cases will go to the City Council for final action

## Decisions & votes

**Case 2026-19: Drive-through bank at 1862 Crystal Parkway**
- **Vote on findings of fact:** Approved 5–0 (Gnewuch/Engelman)
- **Vote on recommendation:** Approved 5–0 (Cantrell/Druckrey) subject to three conditions: compliance with the submitted site plan, submittal of a stamped site plan showing landscaping and lighting before permits are issued, and compliance with all applicable codes

**Case 2026-20: Subdivision ordinance text amendments**
- **Vote on findings of fact:** Approved 5–0 (Druckrey/Cantrell)
- **Vote on recommendation:** Approved 5–0 (Cantrell/Druckrey)

**Minutes from August 11, 2026 meeting**
- Approved 5–0 (Cantrell/Druckrey)

## Discussion items

The commission heard that Build Holding Company will construct a roughly 4,120-square-foot bank branch with three drive-through lanes plus a bypass lane on a 1.2-acre lot in the Belvidere Retail Subdivision. Applicant Evan Pomerance said construction could start by spring 2027 but declined to name the national bank brand. Staff confirmed the nearest residence is 1,200 feet away and headlights from the drive-through will point south toward an existing dispensary. The site will require landscaping under a point system.

On the subdivision ordinance changes, staff explained the amendments close loopholes in state exemptions that have allowed non-conforming lots to be created, reduce inspection fees from five percent to three percent of construction costs, and create a new "plat of consolidation" process to make it easier for property owners to formally combine lots under common ownership. Commissioner Paul Engelman confirmed the consolidation process would help prevent gaps in sidewalks that occur when lots are combined only for tax purposes.

Fourteen residents spoke during public comment opposing a potential data center, though no data center case was on the agenda.

## Financial / operational notes

Inspection fees for public improvements in new developments will be set at three percent of the approved engineer's cost estimate, down from five percent, to match current practice. Any inspection costs beyond three percent must be paid before the city accepts the improvements.
