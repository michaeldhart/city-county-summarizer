## The lede

- The board rejected a proposal to lower speed limits on Rockton Road, McCurry Road, and Grade School Road in Manchester Township from 55 mph to 35-45 mph, with the motion failing on a 6-5 vote after several members said they wanted a speed study done first.
- The board approved $45,323.63 in change orders for the courthouse restoration project, including masonry repair, cornice painting, and sidewalk replacement.
- The board hired the county's IT intern as a full-time employee at $17,500 for the remainder of the fiscal year after IT Director Joe Shaden said the intern had graduated and needed health insurance benefits.
- During public comment, several residents voiced concerns about recent soil boring and surveying activity near their properties south of I-90, asking what projects were planned for the area.

## Attendance & housekeeping

Board member Lisa Patterson arrived at 6:39 p.m. The board approved the July 16, 2026 meeting minutes and general consent items including fund transfers, road projects, acceptance of donations, and raffles. The board appointed Lt. Dan Reilley to the Emergency Telephone System Board and Vicky Brockmann as second alternate to the Zoning Board of Appeals. A proclamation was issued for Constitution Week.

## Decisions & votes

**Change Order #29 for courthouse project: Approved (as amended)**  
The board approved three change orders totaling $45,323.63 (amended to "not to exceed") for: masonry restoration ($4,691.20), cornice painting ($19,000), and sidewalk replacement ($21,632.43). Project manager Sean Darnell said the masonry work was necessary because deterioration was hidden by covering applied in the 1920s. Board member David Shoemaker asked whether the city would share costs on the sidewalk replacement under its 50-50 program. Vote: Johnson–yes, Shoemaker–yes, Cordre–yes, Schneider–yes, Wilty–yes, Patterson–yes, Wahlberg–yes, Riley–yes, Reggano–yes, Logston–yes, Thornberry–yes.

**Ordinance 26-33 (altered speed zones in Manchester Township): Failed**  
Vote: Cordre–yes, Schneider–yes, Wilty–yes, Patterson–no, Wahlberg–no, Riley–no, Reggano–yes, Logston–yes, Thornberry–no, Johnson–no, Shoemaker–no. The ordinance would have reduced speed limits to 35 mph on Rockton and McCurry roads and 45 mph on Grade School Road. Manchester Township Road Commissioner Jim Schwarzbock said the roads are narrow with vegetation close to the edge, increased bicycle and pedestrian traffic, and hidden driveways. He said the township passed a resolution two months ago requesting 45 mph limits on all township roads. Board Chairman (Shoemaker) said he thought it was wrong to change speed limits without doing a speed study first, noting "we are a rural county" and that slower speed limits won't stop people already driving 70 mph. County Engineer Justin Krone said a speed study would take about a month and explained that new state policy allows more flexibility beyond the traditional 85th percentile rule. Board member David Wilty supported the reduction, saying "I don't want to see somebody get killed because we didn't do this." Board member Marian Thornberry opposed it, saying "I can't see any reason to reduce it to 35."

**Ordinance 26-31 (move Regional Planning Commission to Chapter 2): Approved**  
Unanimous voice vote. The ordinance moves the Regional Planning Commission section from Chapter 62 to Chapter 2, Article 7 (Administration) with no change to language.

**Ordinance 26-32 (move Zoning Board of Appeals to Chapter 2): Approved**  
Unanimous voice vote. The ordinance moves the Zoning Board of Appeals from the zoning ordinance to Chapter 2, Article 8 (Administration).

**Ordinance 26-29 (stormwater protection and NPDES permits as Appendix C): Approved**  
Unanimous voice vote. The ordinance moves stormwater and NPDES permit language from the subdivision ordinance to Appendix C with no substantive changes.

**Ordinance 26-30 (floodplain ordinance as Appendix B): Approved**  
Unanimous voice vote. Staff said the language was recommended by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources and is required for flood insurance compliance.

**Claims totaling $1,542,162.91: Approved**  
Vote: Schneider–yes, Wilty–yes, Patterson–yes, Wahlberg–yes, Riley–yes, Reggano–yes, Logston–yes, Thornberry–yes, Johnson–yes (with noted abstentions), Shoemaker–yes, Cordre–yes. Karl Johnson abstained from claims presented by William Charles, Northern Illinois Service Co., Core & Main, the Highway Department, and county departments regarding sewage, stormwater, construction, and excavation.

**Hiring IT intern as full-time employee: Approved (as amended)**  
Vote: Schneider–yes, Wilty–yes, Patterson–yes, Wahlberg–yes, Riley–yes, Reggano–yes, Logston–yes, Thornberry–yes, Johnson–yes, Shoemaker–yes, Cordre–yes. The motion was amended from keeping the intern for the rest of the year at $14,000 to hiring him full-time at $17,500 plus benefits. IT Director Joe Shaden said the intern had graduated college, aged out of his parents' insurance, and had learned the county's systems well over four to five months. Administrator Becky Tobin said the difference would initially come from contingencies but that there was about $5,000-$10,000 in wiggle room in the IT budget. Board member David Wilty noted the employee would also start earning pension credit. Board member Josh Shoemaker said the county should pursue contracts with townships and District 2 for IT services to help pay for the position.

**Courthouse boiler retube project with Rockford Steam Boiler Works for $19,040 (not to exceed $25,000): Approved**  
Vote: Wilty–yes, Patterson–yes, Wahlberg–yes, Riley–yes, Reggano–yes, Logston–yes, Thornberry–yes, Johnson–yes, Shoemaker–yes, Cordre–yes, Schneider–yes.

**Purchasing card for Maria Ramirez in State's Attorney's Office: Approved**  
Unanimous voice vote.

## Discussion items (no vote)

Growth Dimensions Director Ro (last name not given) reported 104 projects in the pipeline, with 35 committed or beginning construction and six completed. Stellantis announced it will begin production of the next-generation Jeep Cherokee on the STLA1 platform in Belvidere in 2028, with full production in 2029—an $800 million investment. Merlin Steel purchased the Franklin Wire facility in July and will meet with Growth Dimensions next month. East Point Commons (Bush Properties/DMD Investments) is being marketed for retail and industrial use. Region One Planning Council received an $8.9 million award for infrastructure solutions including improvements near US Route 20, I-90, and Irene Road. Growth Dimensions is working with the Illinois Board of Realtors, the health department, and planning department to identify housing needs and challenges. The annual Growth Dimensions dinner is scheduled for September 2nd with Walmart Perishable Distribution Center General Manager Robert Leki as keynote speaker.

Senior Connections gave a brief report inviting board members to visit and see activities for seniors.

The chairman explained the development review process in response to public concerns, noting projects go through the planner, planning commission, zoning board of appeals (with public input), administrative committee, and then full board—a process designed to prevent anything being "snuck through."

## Public comment

**Mike Novak**, 4415 Genoa Road, said he has seen soil boring and surveying activity around his farm and expressed concern about planned commercial/industrial development in the area referenced in the county's strategic plan filed in June. He said residents were never contacted or surveyed and asked what is being planned south of I-90.

**Jeffy**, 7272 Huber Road, echoed Novak's concerns and questions about the data center or building, saying "something's going on" and asking about the timeline.

**David Henderson**, 3030 Center Road, Spring Township, said he's concerned about the drilling activity and what would happen to drinking water, roads, and property values if a project occurs. He said he's against it and noted that while he believes the board is telling the truth about not knowing details, "somebody knows something somewhere."

**Scott** (last name unclear), 9176 (road name unclear), questioned why the county would want to build a data center that would make a CEO rich and harm the community. He said agriculture runs the country and asked why the county would use resources to benefit someone living halfway across the country or world. He also criticized the school board for spending $30 million on preschool facilities.

## Notable moments

There were significant technical difficulties with the sound system throughout the meeting. At one point during the Growth Dimensions report, the microphone stopped working entirely, causing a several-minute delay and jokes from board members. One member quipped, "Not the worst day I've had this week," referring to a previous incident involving water. Another joked that unlike doctors and attorneys who get deposed, county officials "sit here and just hold it."

The chairman gave an unusually detailed explanation of the county's development review process at the start of public comment, apparently in response to concerns about a potential data center project. He emphasized multiple times that state law prevents the county from sneaking projects through and that residents could sue if proper procedures weren't followed.

Manchester Township officials brought visual aids and came prepared with detailed measurements and timing data—Road Commissioner Jim Schwarzbock said he timed the drive from county line to Free Church Road at 35 mph and it took two minutes, arguing "if you need to go faster than that, you should have left earlier."

The board learned during the meeting that Public Defender Kate Cross had been appointed as an associate judge for the 17th Circuit and would resign effective September 1st. The chairman noted the board has no control over who the 17th Circuit appoints as her replacement.
