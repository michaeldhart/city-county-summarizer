## The lede

- The committee approved an $14,640.73 change order to add roof insulation to fix drainage issues on the Logan Avenue project after construction revealed a 2-inch height discrepancy between the new and existing roof surfaces.
- Highway Department Director Justin Cone will lead installation of underground stormwater drainage for the McKinley parking lot instead of building detention ponds, after board members rejected the original design as too expensive and maintenance-intensive.
- The board advanced bids totaling over $960,000 for highway projects including a $623,903 bridge replacement on North Boone School Road and a $165,608 live-bottom truck body for snow removal operations.
- Members discussed but took no action on raising county board meeting pay from the current $65 per meeting, which has not increased since it was reduced from $75 in 2008.

## Attendance & housekeeping

The committee opened at 6:03 p.m. Present: Chairman Carl Johnson, Ryan Curry, Brian Schneider, Dave Wiltse, Josh Shoemaker, Tom Walberg, Alisa Patterson, Dana Logsdon, Matt Ragano, Vice-Chairman Rodney Riley, and Marion Thornberry. Absent: Dave Cordray.

Before public comment, Schneider asked for a moment of silence for Bob Grace, who served 27 years with Capron Rescue and nearly 30 years in the military. Wiltse led a prayer.

The committee approved minutes from December 11, 2025 and January 15, 2026 meetings and the agenda as presented.

## Decisions & votes (each with vote count and notable discussion or dissent)

**Change Order #8 - Scandroli Construction roofing ($14,640.73):** Approved by roll call vote (all yes). Architect Clayton Cluber explained the original design assumed the existing roof surface would align closely with the new addition, but when contractors removed the parapet wall they found the new deck sat 2 inches lower than needed. The fix adds 2 inches of rigid foam insulation to align drainage from south to north. Schneider questioned why measurements during design didn't catch the issue. Cluber said no original construction drawings existed for the old grocery store building, forcing them to rely on field measurements which proved accurate but couldn't account for all conditions without "destructive testing" like drilling through the roof.

**Minutes approval (December 11 and January 15, 2026):** Approved (voice vote). Brief confusion when some members initially didn't see documents in BoardDocs.

**Agenda approval:** Approved (voice vote).

**Intergovernmental Agreement with Conservation District:** Approved (voice vote) to move to full board. Agreement allows temporary construction access on Conservation District land south of Woodstock Road for the bridge reconstruction project over the Piscasaw Creek to reduce construction costs.

**Barricade bid - RoadSafe Traffic Systems ($7,705.50):** Approved (voice vote) to move to full board. Purchase includes 15 Type 3 barricades (6 ft x 4 ft), 15 Type 2 A-frame barricades, and 75 LED lights for a new trailer the highway department is building.

**North Boone School Road Bridge - M&M Concrete ($623,903):** Approved (voice vote) to move to full board. Low bid came in substantially below the second bidder and engineer's estimate. Cone reported checking references with Stephenson County, which has used M&M Concrete on multiple culvert projects with good results. Funded 100% by special motor fuel tax Township Bridge Program with no county cost.

**Truck body bid - Monroe Truck Equipment ($165,608):** Approved (voice vote) to move to full board. Base bid for live-bottom truck body to replace the county's last remaining live-bottom plow truck. Cone explained live-bottom trucks use augers to move salt to the spreader without lifting the bed, avoiding overhead wires and trees. The county has shifted mostly to standard dump bodies but wants to keep one live-bottom as a training vehicle.

**Poplar Grove Road paved shoulders Phase I & II - Willet Hoffmann & Associates ($132,772):** Approved (voice vote) to move to full board. Engineering agreement for 2.8 miles of 4-foot paved shoulders (bike lanes) from Village of Poplar Grove on Route 173 to North Boone school campus. Project seeks federal R1 funds and must be built in 2027, requiring fast turnaround.

## Discussion items (no vote)

**McKinley parking lot redesign:** Extended discussion resulted in direction for staff to eliminate detention ponds, have Highway Department install underground drainage connecting downspouts from both buildings to existing storm sewer on McKinley Street, and proceed with RFP for parking lot paving. Chairman Johnson opposed the original design showing three detention ponds, calling them expensive to dig, unsightly, prone to weeds and litter, and a breeding ground for mosquitoes. He suggested dry wells or connecting directly to the 12-inch storm line. Sheriff's Office Administrator Becky Tolen said detention ponds were proposed to maximize green space and offset impervious surface from the new evidence storage building in back, though engineer John Bloomberg said the small addition didn't strictly require detention under county review. Board members agreed stormwater management was critical but preferred preserving parking spaces. Cone said his crews could handle the underground work during good weather while plows are still on trucks. Riley questioned if city participation would affect surface drainage since the property was previously an aggregate trucking yard that likely wasn't porous.

**Access control doors - Audio Engineering ($39,900 estimated):** Tolen presented proposal to add card access doors at McKinley buildings (EMA, Coroner, CORE) and Public Safety Building lower level as an addition to the existing courthouse card access contract. The $49,700 original estimate was reduced after Sheriff Justin Dunne said garage door access ($9,800) was not needed. Coroner requested three doors: front entrance, vestibule interior, and rear entrance to keep public contained. Sheriff explained he hasn't carried keys in 25 years and reverting to physical keys would be "going backwards." Committee will vote next month on the $39,900 amount.

**County board per diem:** Extended discussion with no consensus. Current rate of $65 per meeting has not increased since being reduced from $75 in 2008 during the recession. Tolen said any change must occur 180 days before new term starts (by May) and would only apply to newly elected members, creating a split board for two years. Chairman Johnson argued the rate is far behind comparable counties (Stephenson pays $100) and doesn't reflect the hours spent outside meetings studying materials and investigating issues. Shoemaker supported an increase to attract younger candidates with families. Riley opposed, noting contested primaries show people willing to serve, county employees make less than $21.66/hour, and service should be about duty not money. Patterson agreed a small increase wouldn't incentivize candidates. Thornberry said any raise should apply to all members, but Wiltse clarified state law prohibits changing sitting officers' salaries mid-term. Committee will continue discussion next month.

**PSB window replacement:** Tolen reported the City of Belvidere likely won't participate in the $43,000 window replacement project covering both city and county offices in the shared Public Safety Building, though she hasn't received final word from the city attorney. The majority of the six window banks are in city space; only two serve county offices (Sheriff and office manager). Quote from contractor is good through March. Randy Darnell recommended replacing all windows on the same wall for aesthetic and building performance consistency. Board directed staff to check with city one more month before deciding whether to proceed with county portion only.

**Tax deed property title issues:** Brief discussion after Treasurer Kurt Newport's report. Curry asked about two residents who purchased properties through tax deed sale (handled by R1) and encountered problems obtaining clear title for construction loans. Tolen said the issue is statewide—banks won't lend because there's no guarantee someone won't claim ownership years later. She, Chairman Johnson and State's Attorney discussed possible solutions including liability insurance. Thornberry noted title research before purchase would reveal problems, but Johnson explained tax deed properties don't provide the same title guarantee as standard real estate transactions.

## Public comment

**Aaron Vanderwal** (2942 Country Meadow Lane) requested a four-way stop at the Caledonia and North Caledonia intersection, at minimum while the board considers a roundabout. She said 3,750 cars use the north leg of Caledonia daily, with 600 from her 75-house neighborhood alone. Residents are familiar with the area and would stop. She thanked the board for clearing trees and noted four-way stops reduced accidents at Caledonia and Dawson Lake.

**Juliet Kingston** (12805 Prairie Meadow) echoed support for a four-way stop with rumble strips as the cheapest temporary solution while a roundabout is considered long-term. She said she's an excellent driver in 28 years and wants the opportunity to stop at the intersection. She thanked the board for clearing trees.

## Notable moments

**Opening prayer and moment of silence:** Before public comment, Schneider requested and received permission to honor Bob Grace, his son's father-in-law, who served 27 years with Capron Rescue Squad and nearly 30 years in the military and passed away a week earlier. Wiltse led a prayer and the board held a moment of silence.

**Roofing engineering debate:** Schneider pressed architect Clayton Cluber on how the roof elevation discrepancy wasn't caught during design. Cluber explained at length that without as-built drawings for the 1950s-era grocery store building, they relied on careful field measurements at three points that matched a model from previous architects within an inch—but couldn't drill through the occupied roof to verify deck height without "destructive testing."

**Detention pond pushback:** Chairman Johnson challenged the McKinley parking lot design throughout, saying detention ponds would become "mosquito heaven" filled with "every piece of garbage from every neighborhood around" and "obnoxious" weeds. Engineer Bloomberg noted they'd be sloped at 1% to drain. Johnson countered, "any water that does stay is going to be mosquito."

**"Legal rape" comment:** When contractor Randy Darnell explained a $766 "technology fee" covers web-based project management software (Procore), Schneider asked, "What the hell is a technology fee? You got to have a smart diploma to do glass or what?" and called it "legal rape." Darnell diplomatically replied he wasn't "sure how to respond to that" but confirmed "it's a markup." Schneider: "You got it right the first time."

**Salary debate:** Riley invoked taxpayer concerns and noted four county job postings paid less than the $21.66/hour effective board rate for a three-hour meeting, adding he was asked when first running in 2020 if he'd "vote yourself a raise" and said no. Johnson countered that three hours in chambers doesn't reflect the additional home study time required. Shoemaker, the youngest member, said taking time from his family "feels like you do need something in return" to attract younger candidates. Patterson called a $10-20 increase "peanuts" that won't incentivize anyone.

**Snow plowing concern:** Sheriff's Administrator Tolen revealed the county forgot to budget for snow plowing at McKinley and is "plowing all this in the back with a skid loader," taking salt costs from other budgets. Reducing parking lot size was partly to address equipment limitations.
